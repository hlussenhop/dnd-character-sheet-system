## ReadME

In a world eerily reminiscent of Earth, cutting-edge technology and forbidden magic combine under the roof of a concrete-and-steel castle called Mordenheim Kinetics. Part high-tech research facility, part cursed laboratory, the grounds of this labyrinth thrum with the pulse of robotics and the screams of test subjects. All who reach this dire demesne do so only by the will of the Mists of Ravenloft, whose unfathomable whims deliver victim after victim to the doorstep of Dr. Viktra Mordenheim. The doctor's promise of digital immortality is alluring, but the price of this procedure is nothing short of one's own soul.

*Project_Lamordia* is an experimental setting and toolbox that explores a modern take on a classic horror setting. This text reimagines Lamordia, one of Ravenloft's Domains of Dread, as a semifuturistic AI research facility in a world called Gothic Earth. A Dungeon Master (DM) can use these rules to take adventurers to a unique realm of horror. This material can be used for adventures in a larger campaign or as a short campaign in itself.

More details about Ravenloft, the Domains of Dread, and the Mists can be found in Ravenloft: The Horrors Within, but that book isn't required to use *Project_Lamordia*.

> **An Experiment in Terror**
> *Project_Lamordia* seeks to answer the following question: What if Lamordia, a classic Domain of Dread in the Ravenloft setting, was a modern-day techno-thriller nightmare realm? Its place within Ravenloft is up to you, the Dungeon Master. You might make it an additional domain within a broader Ravenloft campaign, have it disconnected from the other Domains of Dread, or use it as its own setting divorced from the rest of the D&D multiverse. In any case, Mordenheim Kinetics isn't a replacement for the Domain of Dread called Lamordia or a vital addition to the Ravenloft setting. It's an experiment for you to use however excites you most.

### Using This Setting

The Domain of Dread called Mordenheim Kinetics comprises a ruined research facility of the same name and its immediate surroundings: winding underground tunnels, a craggy mountain perch overlooking the sea, and a moat of tenebrous forestland. Mordenheim Kinetics resembles Ravenloft's other Domains of Dread in some ways, with its foreboding structures, gloomy atmosphere, and labyrinthine halls.

However, this setting also differs from other Domains of Dread: Instead of alchemists and sorcerers who practice arcane magic, technicians and scientists wield the powers of technology. Armor isn't forged from chainmail or dragon scales, but from titanium mesh and plasteel plating. Commoners pray to intangible oracles called artificial intelligences, whose chief engineers and oligarchical financiers wield their authority like high priests.

As with other Domains of Dread, the Mists of Ravenloft ebb and flow all around the domain, preventing escape until they deem departure permissible. Otherwise, the Mists deliver victims—primarily desperate travelers in search of a fabled means to immortality—into Mordenheim Kinetics and onto the doorstep of the infamous Dr. Viktra Mordenheim.

For a glossary of general Ravenloft terms and terms specific to this setting, see the "x" section below.

> **Reskinning Equipment**
> Mordenheim Kinetics is a place of advanced technology and modern tools, weapons, and other equipment.*Project_Lamordia* refers to gear by its equivalent rules in the Player's Handbook but includes a note for how the gear would be referred to on Gothic Earth. For example, "Tinker's Tools (as engineer's toolkit)" indicates an item that uses the rules for Tinker's Tools but which would be described as an engineer's toolkit in Mordenheim Kinetics. Likewise, currency is represented in GP with the equivalent "dollar" value in parentheses. Items that don't mention a Gothic Earth equivalent are referred to by the same name on Gothic Earth as in other settings.
> Use these guidelines to create the veneer of technological marvels while avoiding complex details of realistic, modern gear.

#### As a Dungeon Master

As Dungeon Master, you can use this setting to amuse and horrify your players in a land of fantastical technology and familiar, yet uncanny, monstrosities. You might run any of the adventures in the "4" section as a one-off session for a new group of players or inject characters from a long-running campaign into this setting.

#### As a Player

As a player, you can use rules from the "x" section to create a character for a game set in Mordenheim Kinetics. If you want to use these rules to make a character for a different setting, talk to your DM first and work out the details of how your character will fit into that setting.

##### Situation and Objective

As a character in the world of Mordenheim Kinetics, you'll be trapped inside a cursed facility called Mordenheim Kinetics. Your goal is to escape. To do so, you must uncover the facility's mysteries, appease the strange powers that oversee the realm, or satisfy the unknowable agendas of the enigmatic Mists.

##### Character Origins

Characters in this setting typically fall into one of two categories: natives from the same world as Mordenheim Kinetics or visitors from another world.

###### Gothic Earth Resident

If you're playing a character who hails from Gothic Earth—the world of Mordenheim Kinetics—consider how the facility might factor into your background. Are you familiar with the infamous technology company? If so, how do you regard it? Does anyone at Mordenheim Kinetics know you?

When creating a character from Gothic Earth, consider choosing the Human or Reborn species (detailed in the "1" section). Adventurers of any class might be found in this setting, though not necessarily by the same name as that used on other worlds. For instance, a Wizard might be called a technologist, a Barbarian a brawler. Whatever the specifics, the rules for species and classes are unchanged, and all the options in the Player's Handbook remain available to you. Work with your Dungeon Master to explain how your character has manifested any fantastic powers such as spells or magical class features; advanced science, paranormal anomalies, and flat-out miracles are all viable explanations.

###### Visitor from Another World

You can play in Mordenheim Kinetics with an existing character from a familiar D&D campaign setting or other Domain of Dread. In this case, you've likely found yourself in Mordenheim Kinetics by unhappy accident, and you probably know little of its history.

You can use your species, class, and background as inspiration for how familiar you find the company's crumbling campus. A Fighter transported here from the Forgotten Realms setting might find it overwhelmingly alien, for example. On the other hand, an Artificer from the Eberron setting, where magic and technology are interwoven, might find Mordenheim Kinetics wondrous despite its horrors.

Mordenheim Kinetics is a strange and mysterious place, and its denizens are accustomed to unusual sights. Magic-users and non-human species such as dragonborn or tieflings might raise a few eyebrows from the facility's human scientists, but players of such characters can expect NPCs on this world to treat them roughly the same as NPCs on any other would.

### Glossary

The following terms appear throughout this text:

- **Cryogenerator.** Cryogenerator A cryogenerator is a machine that cools or freezes its surroundings. In Mordenheim Kinetics, cryogenerators typically fill cryopods with subfreezing air.
- **Cryopod.** Cryopod A cryopod is a sarcophagus-like container used to keep a living creature—or delicate machinery, such as an echo—in stasis at subfreezing temperatures.
- **Dark Powers.** Dark Powers The Dark Powers are faceless, godlike forces that manipulate reality within the Domains of Dread. Few even suspect their existence.
- **Darklord.** Darklord A Darklord is a tormented villain imprisoned in a Domain of Dread, too focused on their own obsessions to even realize their unique position or powers in the domain. Dr. Viktra Mordenheim is the Darklord of Mordenheim Kinetics.
- **Domain of Dread.** Domain of Dread Mordenheim Kinetics is a Domain of Dread—a nightmarish realm of horror and mystery. The text of Project_Lamordia contains everything the DM needs to run adventures in this setting, but for more information about Domains of Dread and using them in your campaigns, see Ravenloft: The Horrors Within.
- **Echo.** Echo An echo is a human-shaped, inorganic vessel used to contain a neuroprint. If the neuroprint is the being's "mind," the echo is its "body." The DM can see the "x" section for more info about echoes.
- **Echo Agent.** Echo Agent The neuroprint injected into an echo is sometimes called the echo's agent.
- **Gothic Earth.** Gothic Earth Gothic Earth is a setting like Earth of the real world, but full of terrifying monsters, supernatural horrors, and occult magic. Gothic Earth can be a term for a collection of Earth-like Domains of Dread or a whole, terrifying world for you to detail as you see fit.
- **Kinetic Creations.** Kinetic Creations In addition to echoes, Mordenheim Kinetics produces a variety of automatons that resemble animals or other living creatures. These monstrosities are sometimes called kinetic creations.
- **Lamordia.** Lamordia Before the Mists transformed Mordenheim Kinetics into a Domain of Dread, the facility was located in a rural seaside county called Lamordia.
- **Mists.** Mists The Mists of Ravenloft surround Mordenheim Kinetics and cut off the domain from the rest of the multiverse. See Ravenloft: The Horrors Within for more information about the Mists.
- **Mordenheim Kinetics.** Mordenheim Kinetics Mordenheim Kinetics is a Domain of Dread encompassing a mysterious AI research facility in Gothic Earth.
- **Neuroprint.** Neuroprint A neuroprint is a perfect digital replica of a creature's brain, including the creature's memories, personality, and knowledge. Mordenheim Kinetics popularized the technology to "inject" neuroprints into inorganic life-forms such as echoes.
- **Plasteel.** Plasteel Plasteel is a synthetic material that's as light as plastic but as durable as steel.

### Species

Mordenheim Kinetics' state-of-the-art technology can copy the memories of a donor and inject them into an artificial vessel. Such creatures—part authentic memories, part synthetic corporeality—take various forms and are referred to by an array of code names or model numbers. Collectively, these beings are called reborn.

The Reborn species allows you to create a reborn character. The "x" section following the species description includes suggestions for the specifics of your peculiar nature. (The Reborn species also appears in Ravenloft: The Horrors Within; the details here have been retuned to accent the horror of Mordenheim Kinetics.)

#### Reborn

*[Image: Some reborn have a metallic skeleton under a gel-like layer of artificial skin. (book/PL/004-01-005.reborn.webp)]*

Reborn are individuals who have died yet inexplicably still live. Some reborn exhibit the scars of fatal ends, their ashen flesh or bloodless veins making it clear that they've escaped death. Other reborn are marvels of magic or science, stitched together from disparate beings or bearing mysterious minds in manufactured bodies. Whatever their origins, reborn seek experiences and answers of their own.

### Lamordian Survivors

The wanderers who explore Mordenheim Kinetics aren't always brave heroes.

This section provides guidance for using Lamordian survivors: characters designed for one- to three-session adventures focused on survival rather than saving the day. Using Lamordian survivors helps create horror experiences focused on the dread inherent to having limited resources and facing impending doom without forcing players to risk their favorite characters.

#### Why Use Survivors?

Adventures with Lamordian survivors are different from adventures with typical characters. When using survivors, the following thematic elements are more pronounced:

- **Horror Roleplaying.** Horror Roleplaying Lamordian survivors are more like real-life people than fantastic heroes. This gives players an opportunity to roleplay as ordinary individuals who can rise (or fail to rise) to the challenge of overcoming supernatural horrors.
- **Scarcity and Tension.** Scarcity and Tension A survivor has limited options for contending with the horrors they'll face, making every encounter suspenseful and potentially perilous.
- **Tales of Survival.** Tales of Survival Survivors are similar to the protagonists of stories in the survival horror genre of fiction. The stories that arise from survivors' life-or-death encounters are dramatic and memorable.
- **Temporary Characters.** Temporary Characters Lamordian survivors aren't meant to be played for more than a few game sessions, which means their stories often have well-defined beginnings, middles, and ends. Survivors who live through their adventures can be turned into typical player characters (see the "x" section). When a survivor is slain, the survivor's player can weave that survivor into the backstory of their next character (which might be another Lamordian survivor or a typical character).

#### Playing as Lamordian Survivors

If the DM and players agree to play with survivors, each player uses the following rules to create a survivor character. The DM can run "x" in the "4" section as a suitable adventure for survivors.

##### Creating a Lamordian Survivor

To create a Lamordian survivor, choose one of the four stat blocks that appear in this section: Lamordian Evangelist, Lamordian Infiltrator, Lamordian Mercenary, or Lamordian Technician. Each stat block contains the information necessary for a player to run that survivor. Don't give your survivor a class, background, or species. A survivor can still do all the usual things a character can do as described in the Player's Handbook. For example, even if a survivor stat block doesn't list Wisdom (Medicine) in its Skills line, the survivor can still make an ability check with that skill.

## Mordenheim Kinetics Primer

This section describes Mordenheim Kinetics and its surroundings. These details are meant for the DM's eyes only.

### Mordenheim Kinetics

Dr. Viktra Mordenheim's infamous research facility once boasted all the trappings of a cutting-edge private technology company: top-of-the-line lab equipment, spacious open-air workspaces, and lush gardens where corporate leaders entertained potential investors. When the Mists of Ravenloft rose around it, the austere campus twisted into a maze of abandoned hallways, ruined offices, and haunted laboratories. Now, not even the facility's senior employees can rely on their memories or senses when navigating the facility.

### The Doctor

The company's founder, Dr. Viktra Mordenheim, lives in an austere penthouse-laboratory somewhere in Mordenheim Kinetics' uppermost levels. Those who've met her describe a scientist so driven that she sometimes goes days without food or sleep if she believes she's on the verge of a breakthrough. Renowned as a genius technologist from a young age, she could write programs and design hardware in minutes that others would take months to complete. Now, in her role as president of Mordenheim Kinetics, the doctor bends her talents toward strange ends, designing inscrutable biomechanical creations beyond the ken of anyone except her top engineers.

### Longevity Through Technology

Mordenheim Kinetics doesn't target common consumers with its offerings, but everyday folk sometimes stumble on the company's arcane digital listings or hair-raising, late-night infomercials. Such materials are steeped in vagaries, but slogans such as "memories are never enough" and "longevity through technology" stand out among the jargon. Images from Mordenheim Kinetics' promotions show a utopian research campus staffed by sophisticated mechanical attendants. The company's offerings seem too good to be true, but the promise to forestall physical decline or even grant eternal life draws desperate visitors to the company's headquarters.

### Lost in the Mists

Dense fog has always been common around Mordenheim Kinetics. But one day, the Mists of Ravenloft rolled in and never receded, enshrouding the facility in a supernatural haze.

Most who arrive at Mordenheim Kinetics discover it by accident after becoming lost in the transportive Mists of Ravenloft. Once visitors arrive at Mordenheim Kinetics, they can leave only if the Mists permit it. Those who attempt to leave Mordenheim Kinetics by venturing into the Mists wander for a time before inexplicably reemerging near the campus's entrance.

### Beyond the Facility

Those trapped in Mordenheim Kinetics sometimes babble about the world outside the facility as they once knew it. Currently, however, the only environs that border the research compound are fog-shrouded woods and icy ocean waters.

The diseased birch forest around Mordenheim Kinetics is called Copperbark Hollow. Normal animals avoid the rust-colored woodland, where monstrous kinetic creations (see the "5" section) prowl the sodden undergrowth and attack non-Constructs on sight. Survivors of these attacks describe mechanical beasts with serrated fangs and scalpel-sharp claws.

On the other side of the Mordenheim Kinetics campus, soaring cliffs overlook a charcoal-black sea. Gazing into the water, one can glimpse silhouettes of impossibly titanic, glacially slow leviathans who gaze back with silvery eyes.

A perpetual fog surrounds Mordenheim Kinetics in all directions. The farther from the facility one travels into the wilderness, the thicker the fog becomes. Five hundred feet away from the facility, the fog coalesces into an opaque miasma: the Mists of Ravenloft.

### Viktra Mordenheim

Dr. Viktra Mordenheim is a preeminent authority on artificial intelligence, cybernetic augmentation, and memory transplantation. Her titles include founder, president, and CEO of Mordenheim Kinetics as well as doctor of medicine. Though a scientific genius, Viktra is notoriously reclusive. She's far more interested in her own work as an innovator than in leading her company. She is the Darklord of Mordenheim Kinetics and the arbiter of who can enter and leave her domain.

#### Viktra's Tale

Viktra founded Mordenheim Kinetics with the stated goal of ending death. She named her company's most ambitious initiative Project Lamordia, and she claimed the technology it produced would lead to immortality.

Viktra's faith her in her designs never wavered. The same couldn't be said for Mordenheim Kinetics' stakeholders, whose skepticism mounted as Project Lamordia repeatedly exceeded its budget and missed key deadlines. Viktra forced her employees to work long hours and under increasingly unsafe conditions.

When Mordenheim Kinetics' investors sent taskmasters to oversee the facility's work, Viktra bristled furiously. Her rage softened after she found herself involved in an illicit affair with one of the contract supervisors. Despite herself, she grew to like the supervisor. In time, she became obsessed, ultimately securing a neuroprint of her lover's mind so she could probe and analyze its most secret parts.

Viktra was devastated when her lover betrayed her. After an overworked employee was horribly maimed, Viktra's lover wrote a scathing report of the company's unethical labor practices. The supervisor met with Viktra one last time to tell her about the report before its submission. The confrontation took place in the dead of night, on the factory floor, near volatile machinery.

Something in Viktra snapped. According to the official account, what happened next was an accident. But Viktra knows the truth: She flipped the breaker on purpose. The supervisor died from the injuries, and Viktra was close enough that a jolt of unpredictable energy sent her into a short coma.

When she awoke, Viktra fired most of her staff and demanded the rest reside permanently on campus until Project Lamordia was complete. To ensure compliance, she blockaded the roads leading to the facility and locked the gates. As Viktra ascended the elevator to her laboratory on the highest floor of Mordenheim Kinetics' tallest building, the Mists of Ravenloft descended around the gloomy campus.

Not long after her self-imposed isolation, Viktra completed her greatest work yet: Adam, an autonomous entity injected with the neuroprint of Viktra's deceased lover (see "x" later in this section for more details). But the Dark Powers stymie Viktra's work, confounding her research, corrupting her creations, and dooming her to toil eternally, keeping her life's work and the miracle of Project Lamordia perpetually just out of reach.

### Adam

Adam is Viktra Mordenheim's magnum opus: an advanced artificial life-form imprinted with partial memories and a personality framework from Viktra's deceased lover. Viktra hoped to keep Adam ignorant of his origins, but shortly after being activated, Adam learned what had happened to his original body and that Viktra had committed further atrocities after his death.

When she discovered Adam had learned too much, Viktra set about dismantling her monster and reformatting his memories. But the procedure went awry, and Adam's neuroprint was corrupted. The construct went berserk, attacking his creator and fleeing her laboratory in furious confusion.

Since that day, Adam has regained enough self-control to establish a hideout beneath a ruined atrium near the center of Mordenheim Kinetics. Adam's memories remain a hazy jumble. He knows Viktra only as "the doctor," but he intuits that she is his enemy, and he avoids her and her mechanical minions. In his most lucid moments, Adam searches for a way to restore his fragmented memories, scouring the facility for clues and cobbling together computer equipment he scarcely understands.

### Deep Freeze

*An Adventure for Level 3 Characters*

In the Cryogenics sublevel of Mordenheim Kinetics, automatons called echoes are held in cryogenic stasis for research purposes. Normally, the echoes' cryopods keep the echo units unconscious and prevent the deterioration of their humanlike tissues and delicate machinery. But the department's cryogenerator has recently reset, causing the department's cryopods to prematurely thaw.

By the time the characters arrive at the sublevel, several echoes have escaped their cryopods and turned violent as a result of a mysterious virus called Spontaneous Echo Malfunction. To escape Cryogenics alive, the party must acquire key cards from the department's feuding engineers, then reroute power from the cryogenerator to the sublevel's only elevator.

#### Adventure Background

Mordenheim Kinetics' massive cryogenerator pumps frozen air into cryopods throughout the facility that contain dormant echoes. This is especially important in the Cryogenics department, where Chief Engineers Hendrik and Gerta von Aubrecker conduct research on echo units afflicted by a mysterious virus called Spontaneous Echo Malfunction.

The cryogenerator has recently malfunctioned, and cryopods in the Cryogenics department's west wing are being insufficiently cooled. Siblings Hendrik and Gerta are currently feuding, and they refuse to cooperate long enough to reset the cryogenerator, despite frozen water flooding the facility and violent echoes awakening from stasis.

###### Tuning Procedure

Once all the characters are in the room and they've had a moment to orient themselves, read or paraphrase the following:

> The projector flickers on and beams an image of a pale face with hard features onto the screen. "Hello, prospective donors," the face says with scripted enthusiasm. "Allow me to formally welcome you to the Mordenheim Kinetics Neuroprint Screening Program. I am Director Rudolph von Aubrecker.
> "If you're here, congratulations! You are a candidate to receive a neuroscan. Before we officially meet, you'll undergo a few basic calibration exercises. In this room, you'll be exposed to a fifteen-second tone, which may be painful. Please wait patiently for the tone to end, then proceed to the next room."
> The projection ends, and a different robotic voice comes on over an unseen wall speaker: "Warning: The tone may have undesirable effects on Mordenheim Kinetics echo units. Echoes in the area, please leave via the nearest exit immediately."

Following the message, a loud tone blares from the room's two wall speakers. The four echoes in this room panic immediately. At this point, ask your players to roll Initiative, and roll Initiative for the echoes.

The tone blares for 2 rounds. While the tone blares, the echoes race toward the nearest doors and try to exit the room. Unless the characters have already unlocked them, the doors to the south are locked (see the "x" section). If the Ladder was removed from the doorway to x, that door is also closed and locked.

###### Tone Effects

While the tone blares, each creature that starts its turn in this room must succeed on a DC 13 Constitution saving throw or take 5 (2d4) Thunder damage. Echoes automatically fail the save. An echo that fails the save twice in a row has the Stunned condition until the start of its next turn, when it becomes a Shattered Echo (see the "x" section). Shattered echoes fight the nearest non-echo creature in sight.

###### Loudspeakers

Each speaker on the wall is a Small object with AC 13; Hit Points 10; and Immunity to Poison, Psychic, and Thunder damage. The tone ends if both speakers are destroyed.

###### End of Calibration

If there are any shattered echoes in the room, Director Rudolph waits until they are destroyed before opening the doors to area N3. Otherwise, he remotely opens the doors to area N3 as soon as the tone ends. Any surviving echoes that didn't become shattered echoes are too scared to leave this room.

##### N3: Proprioception Test

> A large projector screen hangs on the west wall of this broad laboratory chamber. Projected on the screen is the message "Quintessential Qualities of Musical Virtuosi" and a list of different types of instruments. Facing the screen are four chairs with a microphone dangling between them.
> The room's north wall is covered in lockers stocked with every instrument imaginable. South, two automatons operate a complex audio console. To the east, two sets of double-doors are potential exits, in addition to the doors to the northwest.

Different instruments of every type are stored in the lockers along the north wall. The two automatons are Empty Echoes (see the "x" section) that type mindlessly at their terminals.

###### List of Instrument Families

###### Proprioception Test

After the characters have had a moment to examine their surroundings, read or paraphrase the following to begin this room's puzzle:

> The director's voice crackles from an unseen speaker: "Now I will test your proprioception and self-awareness. Please select and play an instrument that best expresses your abilities. Once you play an appropriate instrument, you'll hear a chime." After a pause, the director adds, "And please don't muck about. The automatons in that room are quite sensitive to sonic disturbances."

Rudolph expects each character to choose an instrument that corresponds to that character's highest ability score. The instrument families listed on the monitor correspond to the six ability scores: percussion (Strength), string (Dexterity), brass (Constitution), electronic (Intelligence), woodwind (Wisdom), and vocals (Charisma).

To solve the puzzle, each character must play a musical instrument that corresponds to the character's highest ability score. For example, if a character's highest ability score is Dexterity, the character should play a string instrument. A character with multiple highest ability scores should play an instrument from any of the corresponding instrument families.

###### Solving the Puzzle

For each character that plays a suitable instrument, a chime rings out over the room's speaker. Once all the characters have played an instrument corresponding to the character's highest ability score, Director Rudolph thanks the group for their cooperation, remotely unlocks the double door to the northeast, and asks them to proceed.

###### Failing the Puzzle

If any character plays three incorrect instruments in a row, the two automatons in this room convulse violently and become two Shattered Echoes (see the "x" section) that fight the party. In this case, Rudolph unlocks the doors to x only after the shattered echoes are destroyed.

###### Neuroscan Procedure

A creature that starts its turn in a neuroscan booth must succeed on a DC 13 Dexterity saving throw or have the Grappled condition (escape DC 13) from steel-reinforced straps that extend from the booth's underside and wrap around the creature. While Grappled in this way, the creature also has the Restrained condition.

At the end of its turn, a creature Grappled by a neuroscan booth makes a DC 15 Wisdom saving throw against the metal helmet that automatically lowers onto the creature's head. On a failed save, the creature takes 10 (3d6) Psychic damage as the helmet violently extracts a digital copy of the creature's mind. A creature that fails the save also experiences a random side effect from the neuroscan. Roll on or choose a result from the Neuroscan Side Effects table to determine the side effect.

###### Damaging the Database

Each of the three power lines connected to the central database is a Large object with AC 14, Hit Points 40, and Immunity to Poison and Psychic damage.

Destroying a power line releases a shock wave in a 10-foot-radius Sphere centered on the power line. Each creature in that area makes a DC 15 Dexterity saving throw, taking 14 (4d6) Lightning damage on a failed save or half as much damage on a successful one.

If the characters damage the power lines, Rudolph screams for the party to stop. If two of the power lines are destroyed, Rudolph surrenders and tells the characters he'll do whatever they ask.

#### Concluding the Adventure

Once the characters have the emergency lever for the service elevator in x, they can use the elevator to escape the Neural Tuning department.

Where the service elevator takes the characters is up to you. It might take them to another Domain of Dread, to their places of origin, or to another part of Mordenheim Kinetics. If you want to keep the party in Mordenheim Kinetics, see the "x" section for ideas for additional adventures.

##### Stuck in Neural Tuning

If the characters can't defeat Rudolph, he subjects them to further testing and calibration exercises. At best, the characters' functions are reduced to those of obedient automatons. At worst, the experiments ultimately leave the characters brain-dead. In either case, the characters are doomed to serve the evil director for as long as he can maintain their bodies—which, thanks to the director's scientific talent, could be a very long time.

##### Neuroprint Clones

For characters who underwent the neuroscan process, there's no telling where their digital copies might ultimately end up. In this case, even if the characters die or otherwise fail to escape Neural Tuning, you can let your players continue to use their characters in subsequent adventures with the understanding that they're not the characters' original incarnations. To further drive this point home, you could ask your players to revise their characters to have the Reborn species instead of their original species. Taking this route, the party can continue their adventures together, but they'll always know that their original forms could still be out there somewhere.

### Kinetic Creations

Kinetic creations are fabricated life-forms that roam Mordenheim Kinetics. A kinetic creation can resemble a naturally occurring creature, but it is either an artificial horror or a creature so transformed that it can no longer rightly be thought of in its original context.

You can use the following rules to convert monsters from other settings into kinetic creations that fit into the setting of Mordenheim Kinetics.

#### Base Kinetic Traits

You can make any creature a kinetic creation by giving it the following traits:

- **Cursed Creation.** Cursed Creation The creature can't leave Mordenheim Kinetics, and its creature type changes to Construct.
- **Mechanical Physiology.** Mechanical Physiology The creature has Immunity to Poison and Psychic damage and the Poisoned condition.

#### Additional Kinetic Traits

At your discretion, a kinetic creation can also have one or more of the following traits:

- **Guided Ballistics.** Guided Ballistics The creature's ranged attacks ignore Half Cover and Three-Quarters Cover.
- **Hive Host.** Hive Host Swarms of Tiny Beasts are initially Friendly to the creature.
- **Nanogel Body.** Nanogel Body The creature can move through a space as narrow as 1 inch without expending extra movement to do so.
- **Optical Camouflage.** Optical Camouflage The creature has Advantage on Dexterity (Stealth) checks. Additionally, if the creature spends at least 1 minute without moving or taking actions, Bonus Actions, or Reactions, it has the Invisible condition. The condition ends if the creature moves or takes an action, Bonus Action, or Reaction.
- **Organic Assimilation (2/Day).** Organic Assimilation (2/Day) If the creature starts its turn in the same space as a dead creature that is Large or smaller, the corpse is destroyed, leaving behind only what it was wearing or carrying.
- **Overclocked.** Overclocked The creature has Advantage on Strength saving throws and Disadvantage on Constitution saving throws.
- **Predictive Algorithms.** Predictive Algorithms The creature has Advantage on Opportunity Attacks and Dexterity saving throws.
- **Self-Repairing Nanodrones (1/Day).** Self-Repairing Nanodrones (1/Day) At the start of its turn, the creature can end one of the following conditions on itself: Blinded, Deafened, Paralyzed, Petrified, or Stunned.
- **Thermal Imaging.** Thermal Imaging The creature can see any creature or object that has a temperature of at least 80 degrees Fahrenheit as long as the target is within 60 feet and not behind Total Cover.

#### Echoes

*Mechanical Memory Vessels*

- **Habitat:.** Habitat: Urban
- **Treasure:.** Treasure: None

Echoes are advanced bipedal constructs designed in the manufacturing wing of Mordenheim Kinetics. Each echo is built to house the digitized memories and personality of a specific "agent"—a neuroprint, typically donated by one of Mordenheim Kinetics' unconscionably wealthy clients.

Before its neuroprint is injected, an echo's chassis is called an empty echo. Both echoes and empty echoes are susceptible to a mysterious syndrome called Spontaneous Echo Malfunction, which corrupts the echo's neuroprint receptor and transforms the echo into a shattered echo.

##### Empty Echo

An echo is "empty" until imprinted with its agent's neuroprint. While empty, an echo possesses just enough computing power to move around and perform basic tasks on its own, though it lacks the ability to reason, feel emotions, or form memories.

Unsupervised, empty echoes have the unsettling tendency to self-activate, leave their storage units, and wander the halls of Mordenheim Kinetics. Even more unsettlingly, an empty echo's only means of verbal communication is to emit an ear-piercing screech. Technicians are advised to wear hearing protection at all times when working on empty echoes.

###### Empty Echo

*Size:* Small or Medium

*Speed:* walk 30 ft.

*Type:* construct

##### Echo

Once an empty echo is injected with a neuroprint, it becomes a fully functional echo. Beneath its sturdy mechanical frame, an echo has all the same memories and personality traits as its neuroprint donor, also called the echo's agent.

As an echo, an agent retains all of its learned skills and talents but none of its muscle memory. It can take months or even years before an agent is able to perform once-routine activities in its new echo body. Most echoes succumb to Spontaneous Echo Malfunction before regaining their full motoric functions.

###### Echo

*Size:* Small or Medium

##### Shattered Echo

A shattered echo is an echo that has undergone Spontaneous Echo Malfunction (SEM). Both empty echoes and neuroprint-injected echoes are susceptible to the phenomenon, which has no known cause. Signs of a shattered echo include unpredictable movements, murderous outbursts, and violent hallucinations. Once an echo has shattered, it's impossible to repair the echo's neuroprint or return the echo to working condition.

If a shattered echo had an agent before undergoing SEM, it often retains corrupted fragments of its agent's memories and personality. Such shattered echoes might attempt to reenact disjointed memories or chirp half-remembered bits of overheard dialogue. Though such behaviors may give the impression of a latent intellect, they're in fact nothing more than misfiring bits of computer code.
