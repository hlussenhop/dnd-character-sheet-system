Since 1974, Dungeons & Dragons has transformed the way people connect around the table. Today, it provides a creative lifeline to millions of gamers around the world, alongside community, laughs, tears, and triumphs. If food is here to nourish the body, then perhaps D&D is here to feed and sustain the imagination. It is no wonder that both dining and D&D so often find themselves at the center of our tables as hubs of community and connection.

In the original Heroes' Feast: The Official D&D Cookbook, we explored how in-world food could elevate the gaming experience, organizing the book around the game's most iconic species and the cuisine they are known for. In the sequel, Heroes' Feast: Flavors of the Multiverse, we were even more ambitious, suggesting D&D food not only enhance your weekly game but also become a centering and essential part of it. In that installment, a group of madcap adventurers dined their way across the multiverse, feasting upon the finest fare of each realm while reminding us of the remarkable range of delicious flavors in our own.

Now, we have proudly cooked up another essential tool for integrating food into your D&D campaigns, your D&D campaigns into your food, and daily life beyond it. In Heroes' Feast: Legends of the Table, we explore the unique cuisine and foodways of each playable class, from their preferred ingredients and preparations to dining habits and presentation. Would a wizard who devotes themselves to the secrets of arcane magic prepare rothé the same way as a ranger who lives off the land? Might the tastes of a cloistered monk differ from those of the barbarian who lives on the frozen tundra? Can an on-the-run rogue or starving bard afford to be choosy with their diets? Perhaps most important, what in the world manifests when a cleric casts Create Food and Water? These, and many others, are the questions that inspired this ambitious quest and the eighty recipes that follow.

Just like the game that inspired it, the Heroes' Feast series is here to help you connect with others even while stoking your own creativity in the most delicious way imaginable. But if there's one thing Dungeons & Dragons has taught us, it's that the rules are just a starting point. They're a framework, not a finish line. In D&D, we customize. We homebrew. We take what's written and make it our own. The same spirit belongs in the kitchen.

So, break the rules. Or bend them. Or ignore them entirely. Recipes aren't commandments; they're invitations. Tinker like an artificer, improvise like a bard, forage like a druid, and season like a fighter with something to prove. Add your own regional flair. Combine flavors no diviner wizard saw coming. And above all, share what you make. Just like a good campaign, meals are meant to be remembered together.

To you and to your friends, families, and party members, we leave you with this blessing from the original *Player's Handbook*: "Enjoy, and may the dice be good to you."

### About This Book: A Feast of Class

Every adventurer brings something unique to the party—and to the plate. In the worlds of Dungeons & Dragons, your class defines your strengths, your spells, your skills, and even your appetites. This unique cookbook explores the cuisine of the classes: Dishes inspired by the lives, traditions, and travels of wizards, fighters, rogues, druids, barbarians, and more.

Some recipes are culled from the menus of legendary inns and fabled strongholds. Others are rustic meals foraged in the wild or ancient delicacies passed down through monasteries, thieves' guilds, and magical colleges. A ranger's stew tastes of campfire smoke and forest herbs, while a bard's feast is spiced with flair and flair alone. A paladin's roast carries the sacred flavor of discipline; a warlock's dessert...well, it may cost more than it appears.

The good news is you won't need a spellbook or divine favor to make any of these recipes. We've swapped out rare planar ingredients for real-world substitutes—so, casting plane shift will not be required. Our culinary team has tested each dish with care, adapting fantasy into something flavorful, accessible, and most importantly, table ready.

Whether you're a seasoned chef or a culinary cantrip-caster, these recipes are designed to be both practical and transportive. They are also organized to help you explore the heart of each class's culture while making sure you don't trip any traps along the way.

As always, we've included headnotes for added lore to help connect the stories of these recipes with the storytelling in your game. You'll also find "Cook's Notes" for additional insights, useful whether you're sautéing at home or preparing a critical hit at your next game night.

#### Tools Proficiencies Required

No dungeon delver ventures forth without a backpack and a torch. And no chef should either (figuratively speaking). While not every dish in this book requires specialized tools, here are some essentials that will help ensure your success and perhaps even unlock a few bonus proficiencies along the way:

- Rimmed baking sheets
- Chef's knives (your finesse weapon)
- Mixing bowls (for rituals of whisking)
- Measuring cups and spoons (precision is a wizard's ally)
- Dutch oven or heavy-bottomed pan
- Cast-iron skillet (especially for open-flame feasts)
- Food processor
- Mortar and pestle (a must-have for druids and potion-makers)
- Stand or hand mixer
- Nonstick skillet
- Muffin tin (yes, rogues bake)
- Pie plate and loaf pans (even the lonely sorcerer craves dessert)
- Garlic press (to keep the vampires away)
- Peeler and pastry brush
- French press or coffee gear (trust us: bards need it)
- Tongs, spatulas, whisks, and turners
- Wire rack and strainer
- Cocktail shaker and muddler (we are all alchemists at heart)

From forging a holiday feast to prepping trail rations for your next foray into the wilderness, the right gear—and the right recipe—makes all the difference.

### Worlds of Flavor

Dungeons & Dragons spans numerous campaign settings, each rooted in a unique world with its own history, mythology, culture...and cuisine. This cookbook explores several of these iconic realms but centers its lens on the culinary traditions of the adventuring classes who call them home—including fighters, wizards, rogues, clerics, and more—each bringing their own flavors to the world's table. Though originally designed for adventure and storytelling, each setting also offers distinctive ingredients, traditions, and techniques that invite culinary discovery. These locales include...

#### Forgotten Realms

The Forgotten Realms is a cornerstone of Dungeons & Dragons, an expansive world shaped by myth, magic, and centuries of storytelling. Created by Ed Greenwood, the setting unfolds across the planet Toril, whose continents include Kara-Tur, Maztica, Zakhara, and the widely explored Faerûn. Beneath it all lies the Underdark, a vast subterranean network teeming with danger and mystery. Faerûn is home to some of the game's most iconic regions and cities: the Sword Coast, Calimshan, Cormyr, Icewind Dale, Thay, and famed ports like Baldur's Gate, Neverwinter, and Waterdeep. The culinary traditions of Toril reflect its cultural breadth, ranging from hearty fare inspired by medieval Europe to vibrant dishes influenced by Asian and Indigenous American cuisines. These foodways evolve not only through the land's diverse peoples but also through the adventurers who reside there.

#### Dragonlance

Born from the imaginations of Tracy Hickman and Margaret Weis, Dragonlance presents a soaring vision of high fantasy—a world of misty woodlands, crystal lakes, snowcapped mountains, and dragons that fill the skies. But beneath its beauty lies constant turmoil. The planet Krynn is shaped by conflict: between gods and mortals, dragons and humanoids, balance and ambition. Magic is scarce, and survival often depends on courage, faith, and the strength of unlikely alliances. The continent of Ansalon, Krynn's most explored landmass, is the backdrop for many of the setting's most notable tales, from the gleaming towers of Istar to the treetop homes of Solace, the strongholds of Solamnia, and the haunted ruins of Xak Tsaroth. The food of Krynn mirrors its diversity: rustic tavern stews, elven delicacies from the forests of Silvanesti, and bold flavors inspired by Eastern traditions, all woven into the daily life of adventurers and common folk alike.

#### Spelljammer

Conceived by Jeff Grubb, Spelljammer launched Dungeons & Dragons beyond traditional fantasy realms and into the cosmos. An audacious blend of swashbuckling adventure and spacefaring wonder, it introduced magical ships—spelljammers—that sail the Astral Sea, linking distant worlds and allowing characters to journey between settings once thought separate. This imaginative leap not only expanded the scope of gameplay but opened a gateway to boundless celestial destinations, wild new adversaries, and colorful interplanetary intrigue. Exotic ports like the Rock of Bral, a bustling planetoid orbiting Toril, capture the setting's heightened space opera flair. In the expanse of Spelljammer, cultures collide, and cuisine evolves into something utterly unique, where the next meal might come from a forgotten moon or a traveling tavern crewed by autognome gastronomes.

#### Greyhawk

Greyhawk is the original campaign setting of Dungeons & Dragons, envisioned by co-creator Gary Gygax as a grounded world of perilous dungeons, arcane secrets, and medieval intrigue. Set on the planet Oerth, a land shaped by oceans, continents, and feuding powers, its most storied region is the Flanaess, a cultural crossroads dotted with legendary cities and perilous wilds. Kingdoms like Keoland, Veluna, the Shield Lands, and Furyondy stand alongside storied sites such as the Free City of Greyhawk, White Plume Mountain, the Village of Hommlet, and Blackmoor. Magic exists here, but it is often feared, regulated, or forbidden, lurking in tombs, towers, and war-torn borders. The setting's sensibilities draw heavily from feudal Europe, especially Britain, imbuing the cuisine with rustic charm: trenchers filled with stew, fire-roasted meats, and ale-battered fare meant to fortify adventurers before the next delve. With its earthy tone and emphasis on mortal struggle, Greyhawk remains a foundational setting, one where survival is hard-earned, and comfort can be found in the glow of a campfire.

#### Eberron

Eberron is a war-scarred world powered by innovation, prophecy, and intrigue, where magic meets machinery. Conceived by Keith Baker, the setting reimagines high fantasy through a pulp-infused, steampunk lens: Airships drift between cities, sentient constructs walk among mortals, and arcane energy fuels everything from streetlamps to lightning rails. At the heart of it all lies the continent of Khorvaire, once united under the Kingdom of Galifar and now fractured into the Five Nations: Aundair, Breland, Karrnath, Thrane, and the devastated Mournland. These differences are reflected in Eberron's cuisine, which is as daring as it is refined. In Sharn, sky-high kitchens fuse charm and alchemy. In Karrnath, bold, hearty meals sustain a nation hardened by war. Eberron's cuisine spans from humble field fare to enchanted, gear-crafted delicacies, all yielding flavors as bold and unconventional as the adventurers who call this world home.

#### Planescape

At the crossroads of belief and reality lies Sigil, an ever-shifting city perched atop the Spire at the center of the Great Wheel. Suspended on the inner curve of a floating torus, this "City of Doors" connects to every plane through countless portals. Designed by David "Zeb" Cook, the Planescape setting granted D&D a home for its most surreal and far-reaching ideas. First hinted at in Jeff Grubb's *Manual of the Planes* (1987), the Spire and Outlands came fully to life seven years later, offering players a world where factions clash over ideology and belief alone can reshape existence. In Sigil, contradiction is law: Gods are barred, yet street prophets alter reality through sheer conviction. Buildings spiral skyward only to vanish into shadowed alleys. Cultures collide, mix, and mutate. This tension extends to its food, where flavors are born of barter and belief. No two meals in Sigil are ever the same...and no appetite returns unchanged.

#### Plane Food

The Dungeons & Dragons multiverse spans more than just worlds; it unfolds across an intricate web of planes—entire dimensions layered around the Prime Material Plane, the baseline "reality" countless adventurers call home. Some of these planes are echo-like reflections, tethered closely to the Material Plane. Others stretch far beyond, homes to deities, devils, and dreamscapes. Each realm, no matter how arcane, carries its own customs and, of course, cuisine.

##### The Feywild

Also known as the Plane of Faerie, the Feywild is a luminous mirror of the Material Plane, aglow with eternal twilight and lush with fantastical flora. It is a realm of magic unbound rife with passion, mischief, and emotional extremes. Fey creatures, harengon (rabbit folk), and ancient spirits call it home. Here, every sense is heightened—colors more vivid, music more stirring, and food more intense. In the Feywild, a meal is never just sustenance; it is a story, a temptation, or a trap.

##### The Shadowfell

The Shadowfell is the Feywild's bleak twin: a grayscale echo of the Material Plane, steeped in despair. This land of silence and decay houses the undead, the forgotten, and the cursed. Hope does not flourish here, and neither does flavor. Food here is bland and joyless, a pale imitation of what it once was. Among the mist-shrouded ruins lurk the Domains of Dread, self-contained demiplanes ruled by dark lords, where fear takes physical form and escape is all but impossible. Whatever food is harvested here is as colorless and drained as the realm itself.

##### Ravenloft

The most infamous of the Domains of Dread, Ravenloft centers on the haunted land of Barovia and its vampiric overlord, Count Strahd von Zarovich. Conceived in 1983 by Tracy and Laura Hickman, Ravenloft has become a legend in its own right—a gothic nightmare enshrouded in fog and tragedy. Accessible only through the Shadowfell (and often unwillingly), its dreary atmosphere clings to everything: the architecture, the weather, and the food. Meals are heavy, often blood-tinged or brined, and best eaten with one eye on the door. In Barovia, every dinner could be your last.

#### Beyond

The culinary adventure doesn't end with the worlds detailed in these pages. The greater Dungeons & Dragons multiverse stretches endlessly outward, encompassing realms as wild and wondrous as imagination allows. There's Athas, the sun-blasted world of Dark Sun, where magic drains the land itself and food is forged from grit and survival alone. There's Strixhaven, the interplanar university where magical disciplines are matched only by cafeteria innovation, and even the desserts are enchanted. From the towering guildhalls and floating spires of Ravnica, where food follows function and faction, to the Radiant Citadel, a sanctuary of cultures from across the Ethereal Plane, the worlds of D&D remain as vibrant and varied as the people who play within them. Each realm offers its own tastes, traditions, and table, and although we can't visit them all here, their flavors echo through every feast born of adventure.

## Barbarian

> Let tonight be a time of song and drink to celebrate our coming victory.
> — Heafstaag, King of The Tribe of the Elk

*[Image: image (book/HFLotT/Barbarian Header.webp)]*

*[Image: image (book/HFLotT/Barbarian.webp)]*

Across the known worlds, barbarians are fierce warriors shaped by the unforgiving lands they call home. While a lone barbarian is formidable, a clan in full fury is a force of nature. Barbarians walk several different paths that imbue their lives with deeper meaning. From the Berserker's adherence to raw violence to the Wild Heart's ability to manifest kinship with animals, or the World Tree's unique ability to tap into cosmic vitality to the Zealot's union with their god while raging—these paths shape their worldview. In all cases, rage is the power that enhances their senses and makes them adept in battle.

XPHB is a deeply personal concept to the barbarian, and each interprets it differently. Some view it as a primal connection to the suffering of the world—an uncontrolled expression of wild magic. Others personify their rage as a revered ancestor or wild spirit or even as the true manifestation of their deepest selves. Yet beyond rage and battle, these wild folk are rooted in rich traditions centered on food, drink, and celebration.

#### Feast, Fire, and Fury

From the frosty tundra of Faerûn to the primal frontiers of Khorvaire and windswept steppes of Krynn, barbarian cultures live in close harmony with the land. Known to be fueled by their rage, barbarians carry this same zeal into all aspects of their foodways, from hunting and foraging to preparation and feasting. Food is never just sustenance; it's a primal expression of identity.

For the barbarian, feasting by fire is a shared tradition. From Icewind Dale to Droaam, the communal meal is sacred; it is a time to celebrate kinship, honor spirits, and give thanks for survival. These gatherings mark seasonal shifts, spiritual trials, and the strengthening of bonds between people and the wild. Across the great expanse of worlds, food and drink serve as sacrosanct rites, marking thresholds of transformation and communion with the forces that shape them. A young warrior might consume the heart of their first kill; a tribal leader may fast for days before imbibing Jhuild to gain clarity. In some clans, only those who endure Fire Root Stew are deemed strong enough to join the warband.

#### Many Worlds, One Flame

Though the barbarian tribes of the multiverse span wildly different realms, their cultures are bound by powerful ties.

On Toril, barbarian tribes roam Faerûn's frontiers, from the Silver Marches and Icewind Dale to the Great Glacier. Among the Reghedmen of the north, a proud, nomadic people living beyond the Spine of the World, meat is a staple: Boar, elk, reindeer, seal, and snowshoe hare are hunted and preserved by smoking or freezing. Glacier Jerky, salted elk or yeti meat frozen into chewable rations, offers portable proteins for their high-energy lifestyle. Raw fish, pine nuts, lichen, fermented vegetables in seal fat, and whale blubber provide vital winter nutrients. These well-organized tribes live in tented encampments and cook over fire. Hoarding is taboo, and hunters provide for all, often in honor of Auril, the Frostmaiden. Reghedmen celebrations follow the rhythm of the seasons, marked by multiday feasts. For example, the Feast of Sun's Return, heralding spring's first sun, is celebrated with sun effigies burned in bonfires and communal platters of hearty dishes such as Reghed Venison Pot Roast.

To the south, the nomadic Uthgardt roam the Savage Frontier. Known for aggression, these totem-worshippers perform meat-rich rituals to commune with ancestral spirits. Children of the open plains who are skilled in pit cooking, they survive on bison or Rothé Steaks, accompanied by root vegetables, wild grains, horse milk, honey, and Ashbread, a flatbread baked in embers with wild herbs.

Near Toril's equator, barbarian cultures flourish in a vastly different ecosystem: The lush, perilous jungles of the Chultan Peninsula and its tropical neighbors. Amid rainforests teeming with predators, their cuisine includes snake, insects, fish, yams, cassava, coconut, firefruit, and dinosaur meat. Cooking methods like volcanic rock grilling and banana-leaf steaming reflect an intimate knowledge of the land.

On the war-torn planet Krynn, the Ice Barbarians, Plainsfolk, and Khalkist Nomads uphold spiritual traditions shaped by gods and elemental forces. In the frozen north, the Ice Folk treat feasting as sacred. Their diet consisting of seal fat, dried kelp, snow berries, and wind-whale blubber requires little cooking. Varkaan, a sacred brew of snowmelt, powdered bone, fermented lichen, and moonspice, is reserved for warrior rites and funerals.

Across the Abanasinian Steppe, the Plainsfolk favor simple but meaningful meals. Their festivals honor spirits and mark the return of lost souls, blending food, fire, and oral tradition. Clan gatherings feature roasted game, corn cakes, berry jams, and tallow-fried breads, washed down with Sunmilk, a fermented goat's milk sweetened with blossom syrup. Effigies of sorrow are burned, and ancestral tales dating before the Cataclysm are chanted by firelight.

In the rugged Khalkist Mountains, the Khalkist Nomads herd goats and sheep for milk, meat, and cheese. Expert hunters and foragers, they subsist on wild game, hoppers (rabbits), sky-singers (birds), and alpine herbs. Mobility is key, so they rely on cured meats and hard cheeses that endure long journeys. Their humble but hearty feasts center on rich, travel-ready stews and open-fire feasts such as Seared Boar and Potatoes.

On Eberron, barbarian clans roam the planet's vast wilderness. These powerful peoples share a deep spiritual bond with primal forces, druidic sects, and the elemental planes. Most tribes hail from the Eldeen Reaches, the Shadow Marches, or the wilds of Droaam. Eberron's barbarians are seasoned warriors who live in harmony with their environment.

Within the Shadow Marches, skilled barbarian chefs prepare meals over greenflame, fires infused with primal magic. Around council fires, they share pots of Blackroot Stew and mugs of Thar'kaal, a glowing drink made from ghostbulb fungus, said to invite ancestral spirits.

In the Eldeen Reaches of Western Khorvaire, dense forests shelter barbarian tribes, many of them shifters, long descendants of lycanthropes who embrace their primal nature. To them, food is a blessing from the land. During midsummer Moonrise Hunt, tribes hunt enchanted forests, culminating in a moonlit feast where shamans chant tales and summon totemic spirits with drumming and dancing.

Far to the east, on the Dragon-continent of Argonnessen, the Dragonbone Reavers reign. Their feasts fuel both battles and dreams, and whether gnawing on Smoked Wolf Thigh or sipping Fermented Mushroom Brew beneath the stars, every bite reflects their fierce and extreme way of life.

#### The Clan Eternal

Untamed by planet or environment, the barbarian spirit roams free. Their feasts are rituals of meaning, their drinks anointed with the power of the divine, and their festivals anchored in cycles older than empires. More than mere berserkers, the barbarians are the keepers of story, guardians of tradition, and living links to the wild forces that shape their worlds.

## Bard

> Holga doesn't love to be disturbed while she's eating her potatoes, kind of the highlight of her day.
> — Edgin Darvis, Harper and Bard of Faerûn

*[Image: image (book/HFLotT/Bard Header.webp)]*

*[Image: image (book/HFLotT/Bard.webp)]*

Bards are the true artists and entertainers of the multiverse. They use their natural charms and talents as performers alongside their penchant for illusory magic to lighten moods, frighten enemies, and even inspire allies in battle. Sometimes called a "XPHB," the bard dabbles in a little of everything from magic and swordplay to storytelling, song, and dance. Most are fueled by the belief that the multiverse was spoken into being by "XPHB," and they carry that tradition forward through the inspired creations of their own, from performance and poetry to the food that they prepare and consume. Of course, any good bard will insist that their cooking is every bit as inspired as the very words they speak and sing.

#### Jack of All Trades

The talents of the bard are as broad and diverse as the multiverse they inhabit. Most belong to a loose association of mentors and peers known as a "college," which aligns with their beliefs and even helps them define and hone their talents. These affiliations not only inspire their artistic creations but equally influence their culinary tastes and gifts. For example, a bard who adheres to the College of Creation and celebrates the cosmos as a work of art might favor clean, natural flavors and ingredients, while a bard of the College of Eloquence might prefer highly complex gastronomic creations worthy of the sophisticated wordplay and rhetoric they espouse. With those who are rooted in the College of Glamour, presentation is paramount as they create foods meant as much to feed the eyes as the belly, while those who follow the College of Lore view every ingredient and dish as an opportunity to tell a story. Curious what's in the Sword Coast Bouillabaisse? Grab a flagon of Sweet Butter Ale, sit back, and get ready for a tale.

When it comes to the preparation of fine cuisine, it is often bards of the College of Swords who use their exceptional skill with the blade to butcher and prepare the best cuts of meat. Meanwhile, those who come from the College of Valor carry their obsession with bravery into their culinary concoctions, which can lead to exquisite flavor combinations or, just as often, dismal culinary failures. In all cases, the bard's focus and expertise with arcane magic is useful in their ability to not only follow and create complex recipes but also aid in food preparation through spells like mage hand and prestidigitation.

#### Inspired Ingredients

Bards of the multiverse are nothing if not resourceful. Whether it be circumstances or ingredients, the scrappy bard will always find a way to make the best out of any situation—or make lemonade out of lemons, as they say in Amn. It follows then that the preferred pantry of the bard is anything they run across that might add a bit of interest or drama to their food. A random encounter with vallenwood root along the trails of Ansalon or a rare fire spice from the markets of Sigil might lead to an inspired dish worthy of song.

However, for a bard to be fulfilled, they usually need to be where the audiences are, such as taverns, eating houses, public squares, and marketplaces. That means the general fare of the bard is geared to the broadest palates, favoring the foods of taverns, inns, and street vendors. Common root vegetables, grains, fish, poultry, beef, and rothé are typical ingredients for the traveling eclectic but are always prepared with a bit of flourish and flair. Bards are also masters of atmosphere, so they recognize any dish can be enhanced with the right aesthetic, music, or conversation. The goal is not merely flavor, but rather to deliver an overall culinary experience that the diner won't soon forget.

#### The Multiversal Language

Music has often been described as the multiversal language, but many believe that food is more deserving of the moniker. Whatever the case, bards recognize that both can unite, inspire, and stir people's souls wherever their travels may take them, which includes virtually every corner of the multiverse.

On Toril, many bards can be found wandering the northern parts of Faerûn and the Dalelands, searching for adventure, wealth, love, or simply a willing audience to entertain. They often find tasty ingredients and spices along the way, sampling the best the world has to offer, from the Traveler's Stew of the Yawning Portal to the Cherrybread of the Elfsong Tavern in Baldur's Gate. Known as forever optimists and hopeless romantics, many Faerûnian bards find themselves inspired to use their talents for a greater purpose, serving virtuous and secretive organizations such as the Harpers, or their insidious and clandestine counterparts, the Zhentarim.

Bards who roam the reaches of Abanasinia on Krynn are bound to happen upon some of the greatest tavern-fare across the multiverse. Legendary establishments such as the Inn of the Last Home serve up inspiring food and drink like mouthwatering Tavern Steaks and Tika's famous Honey Mead even as bards earn their dinner by regaling guests with stories by the hearth. Those who make their way to the northern reaches of Solamnia to the City of Kalaman will delight in Tamarind Balls found in the bazaars or fresh Steak of the Deep on offer at the Sea Steed tavern.

In the City of Greyhawk, on the world of Oerth, shadowy bards of the College of Whispers can be found skulking in corners of the Green Dragon Inn supping on Chocolate Scones with Yak Butter while eavesdropping on the hushed conversations of adjacent booths. More restless bards might be spied roaming the world's country hamlets preparing a roadside Hommlet Omelet made from fresh goose, duck, or quail eggs.

#### Foods of Inspiration

No matter where their travels take them, bards will always surround themselves with food that is more than meets the eye. As cooks, they will scrounge up a scrappy celebration of flavors, illusions, and distractions that make for an unforgettable dining experience. As diners, their tastes necessarily follow their travels, which is anywhere a gracious audience can be found.

## Cleric

> If any is the pet, I fear it is I. Percival accepts my friendship—sometimes—and my food—rather eagerly.
> — Cadderly Bonaduce, Scholar-Priest Of Deneir

*[Image: image (book/HFLotT/Cleric Header.webp)]*

*[Image: image (book/HFLotT/Cleric.webp)]*

Clerics are the divine stewards of the multiverse, charged not only to serve their gods but to nourish the bodies and spirits of those around them. Whether tending to the sick, guiding the faithful, or standing firm in the face of cosmic evil, clerics channel divine energy into the world and often into their kitchens. For many clerics, the act of preparing food is an extension of their sacred rites: a ritual of hospitality, healing, and humble devotion.

#### Divine Purpose

Cleric domains channel the countless expressions of the gods' will, each one reflecting a distinct facet of the divine—wrath and mercy, light and shadow, war and wisdom. Life Domain clerics are living embodiments of restoration and care, while War Domain clerics stride boldly into conflict, bolstering allies with righteous fury. Light Domain clerics act as beacons against darkness, wielding radiance as both shield and judgment, whereas Grave Domain clerics traverse the shadowed edge between death and peace. Trickery Domain clerics delight in subversion and sacred mischief, while Knowledge Domain clerics preserve the lore of the cosmos itself. Even within the same pantheon, two clerics may differ wildly in outlook and method, each channeling the divine through their chosen creed. But regardless of their domain, clerics bring unwavering dedication and focus to the table.

#### Sacred Sustenance

Where wizards measure grams and glyphs, clerics measure compassion. Their food is rarely about flair; it's about meaning. Meals prepared by a cleric often carry symbolic weight, draw from ancient temple traditions, or serve as a vehicle for blessings. In the hands of a cleric, a loaf of bread is more than sustenance—it's an offering, a ward, or even a vessel for divine magic.

Clerics of healing deities such as FRHoF or FRHoF often brew restorative broths rich in garlic, bone, and curative herbs, passed down through temple kitchens for centuries. Worshippers of FRHoF, the Grain Goddess, might create multigrain harvest loaves blessed to stay fresh for weeks. Followers of SCAG, the dwarven "All-Father" who embodies the unbreakable spirit of dwarvenkind, craft hearty meals meant to strengthen the body for labor and forgework, often accompanied by a strong dwarven ale.

Some clerics imbue their cooking with divine magic courtesy of low-level spells like purify food and drink or ceremony, which bestow benefits of cleansing or healing benefits. Similarly, the effects of a lesser restoration or remove curse spell might be delivered via food. And where no nourishment exists at all, a skilled cleric can conjure it forth via create food and water in a quantity to feed a small village. Heroes' feast, a more advanced cleric casting, offers powerful resistances, immunities, and other boosts on the eve of battle to twelve fortunate partakers. Despite their varied invocations, clerics often initiate the joy of sharing a meal with those in need, an act of service that is, in many faiths, its own form of prayer.

#### Feasts of Faith

Clerics' culinary traditions vary widely depending on their pantheon and plane of origin. In Faerûn, temples commonly operate public kitchens as part of their divine charge. In Baldur's Gate on feast days, devotees of FRHoF serve Blessed Luck-Cakes, which are coin-shaped pastries filled with dates and honey that supposedly bestow a day's good fortune. In Waterdeep, faithful priests of FRHoF serve celebratory Wedding Soup after performing the sacred nuptials. In the cloistered chapter houses of FRHoF, the Binder of Knowledge, clerics craft Scroll Soup, which has noodles inscribed with prayers in edible ink and is consumed in reverent silence, the words becoming part of the worshipper's body and mind.

On Krynn, the followers of DSotDQ (patron deity to the gods of Good) uphold sacred feasting rites during high holy days, including the Feast of Gratitude, featuring Roast Gammon Pie, a comfort dish so divine that it transcends location, unbound by plane or realm. In stark contrast, cults of DSotDQ or DSotDQ may host symbolic meals of blackened roots and blood-colored wines, meant to remind followers of death's ever-present reach. These meals are sparse and ritualized, serving more as devotional ordeal than nourishment.

In Eberron, where faith intersects with invention, divine meals are often portable and efficient, yet still sacred. The followers of ERLW produce Emberbread, a dense, flame-kissed biscuit meant to sustain for days. Said to "burn evil from within," Emberbread is a common ration for itinerant clerics. In Sharn's sky-temples, the faithful of ERLW serve Sanctified Pottage ladled from brass cauldrons, available to anyone in need regardless of creed. Blessings are expressed between each serving, brief prayers woven into the act of giving. Boldrei's Feast, a holiday on the ninth of Rhaan, is a communal feast celebrated across Khorvaire overseen by her many devout clerics.

Amid the endless convergence of pantheons in Sigil, the City of Doors, the food of the faithful is astonishingly diverse. Wandering clerics of FRHoF offer Barovian Garlic Bread imbued with restorative magic said to reinvigorate the sick and ward off the undead. In Sigil, one can dine on gnomish fermented fungus stew, sacred elven fasting breads, or a celestial Holiday Rib Roast (this page) prepared in the upper halls of Mount Celestia, all within a few blocks, if you know the right door to knock on.

#### The Spirit in the Meal

For clerics, food is never just food. It is a sacrament, a symbol, a tool of healing and faith. Where wizards dazzle, clerics comfort. Where druids enchant, clerics bless. They believe the divine can be tasted in a warm slice of bread, whispered in the fragrance of steeping herbs, and shared with anyone who finds themselves hungry in body or soul. Across the multiverse, no matter the deity or domain, the table is a holy place. And when a cleric serves a meal, it is an invitation to experience the divine one bite at a time.

## Druid

> If you must protest, I respond only to hunger strikes and, even then, too late.
> — Jaheira, Druid of The Circle of the Land

*[Image: image (book/HFLotT/Druid Header.webp)]*

*[Image: image (book/HFLotT/Druid.webp)]*

Passionate protectors of nature, druids are the allies and nurturers of all that is natural in the multiverse. Sometimes called "Nature Priests of Primal Power," they live among forests, jungles, and other locales unspoiled by industry and civilization. Rather than trying to master the wilds, druids consider themselves an extension of it, using their wiles, survivalist grit, and substantial natural magic to serve as nature's stewards and sometimes even its wrath. Whether followers of nature-oriented gods or nature itself—called the "Old Faith"—druids believe that there is a natural form, function, and process in everything, and food is a necessary part of that cycle. Consequently, druids choose and prepare foods that are natural, wholesome, sustainable, and minimally disruptive to whatever local ecosystem they find themselves in.

#### One with Nature

When a druid dwells among nature, they are never without. Every leaf, branch, insect, and animal plays a part in the natural order, and the druid both protects that balance and sustains themselves within it. Not only are they exceptional foragers due to their knowledge of edible plants, herbs, and spices, but their connection to the wild makes them wily trappers and hunters when the need demands. This is especially apparent when in XPHB, where they assume the form, talents, and sometimes tastes of their beast allies. For example, a druid who seeks larger game for a hungry party stranded in the wilderness might assume the form of a panther to track a fleet-of-foot elk.

Druids can also use their nature-powered magic to lend a hand in sourcing and preparation. While few druids eat meat, those who do can use simple incantations such as snare to catch their prey and then create bonfire to quickly cook up the meal. They can also use their powers to commune with and find animals and plants to aid in sourcing. In desperate cases, some druids have been known to conjure a swarm of insects not to overwhelm enemies but to provide a simple, high-protein meal for a party of starved adventurers. In all cases, humane and sustainable sourcing is paramount for the druid.

#### Nature's Cupboard

The tastes and ingredients of the druid are highly dependent on both their locale and their personal beliefs. Virtually all druids would be inclined to use a combination of sustainable and locally sourced plants, herbs, and spices in their everyday cooking, but many gravitate to ingredients and flavors from flora they consider sacred, such as juniper, oak, and birch. In hotter, dryer climes, cactus plants and yucca trees hold a similar reverence, while various mushrooms are celebrated in many areas of the Underdark and Feywild.

However, the most significant factor that guides the druid's diet, ingredients, and foodways is their loosely knit society known as a "circle," which supports and influences their specific beliefs. For example, a member of the Circle of the Land is driven by protecting ancient traditions and knowledge, which includes sacred rites, festivals, and the recipes that go along with them. Meanwhile, those of the Circle of the Moon are fierce protectors of the deepest wilds and are less inclined to eat meat or make any impacts on the natural ecosystem. The druids of the Circle of the Sea revel in the powers of the wind and water and are inclined to sustainably forage their meals from oceans, lakes, and rivers, while those of the Circle of the Stars have tastes as diverse as the very cosmos they gaze upon.

#### The Nature of the Multiverse

Nature comes in many forms across the multiverse. As druids can be found on every realm, it follows that they are as diverse as the very natural habitats they call home, as are their customs, beliefs, and foodways.

Druids of the planet Oerth are most inclined to worship nature itself as adherents of the Old Faith. However, it is not uncommon for these forest dwellers to also venerate nature-oriented gods such as XDMG, the Oerth Mother, as well as XDMG, XDMG, and XDMG. From the Burneal Forest of northern reaches of the Flanaess near Blackmoor to the Dreadwood of Keoland in the south, druids of this world roam their sacred wilds gathering fruits, vegetables, herbs, and spices to make their own variations of Everything Soup. Those who are in search of effective healing remedies might be on the lookout for plump berries, which they can transmute into goodberries for use in a tasty Goodberry Blend elixir.

The continent of Faerûn of the planet Toril is teeming with druids in its myriad forests, jungles, and grasslands unspoiled by the engines of industry. They venerate the nature gods of the "First Circle," who include FRHoF; FRHoF; FRHoF; and even gods of nature's destructive power such as FRHoF, FRHoF, and FRHoF. Druids of the High Forest live in harmony with local elves, often sharing in a fresh bowl of Wild Strawberry Salad. The druids of Neverwinter Wood often commune with nature-loving Fey creatures who frequently emerge from the local Fey Crossing bringing with them their legendary Feywild Eggs, making the dish a favorite of druids on both sides of reality.

The druids of Eberron are less inclined to recognize that world's commonly held pantheon of the Sovereign Host or the Dark Six and instead believe that every element of nature, from the sun and stars to the soil and fire, has its own spirit. This belief carries over to ingredients and food as well. While each sect, from the Gatekeepers and Children of Winter to the Ashbound, has their own philosophy on their roles as protectors, they all agree that their natural magic is a way to command and communicate with these spirits and that certain recipes, such as Root Vegetables, Gratin-Style, are not only delicious but help the spirits coalesce.

The druids of Abanasinia of the world Krynn often share the forests with the nature-loving Qualinesti elves of the west and Silvanesti elves of the east and recipes along with them. Qualinesti Vegetable Stew has since become a favorite of druids not just across the planet but the multiverse.

#### The Circle of Food

No matter where you find them, from the lushest forests of Ansalon to the deepest jungles of Chult, druids are protectors of the world around them, even while exposing and cultivating some of its most extraordinary natural flavors. They use their substantial practical and magical talents of nature to source foods and ingredients that are fresh, clean, sustainable, and delicious. While their cuisine is as diverse as the worlds they traverse, it can be all but assured that a party with a druid on board will never go hungry in the wilderness.

## Fighter

> Yer pardon, fair lady. Might I be getting a bit o' food?
> — Bruenor Battlehammer, Fighter and King of Mithral Hall and Gauntlgrym

*[Image: image (book/HFLotT/Fighter Header.webp)]*

*[Image: image (book/HFLotT/Fighter.webp)]*

Fighters are masters of the battlefield. Experts of arms and armament, these fearless warriors typically inhabit the front lines of combat, charging bravely into battle using a combination of martial skill, tactics, and brawn to win the day while gathering the well-earned spoils of war in the process. Among the most diverse of adventuring types, fighters come from all backgrounds and walks of life, from knights and soldiers to gladiators and mercenaries. However, one thing they all have in common is a need to eat, and fighters across the multiverse have proven that they have not only a taste for combat but also for delicious food. For the fighter, the rules of dining are simple: Satiate the appetite, nourish the body, and save the celebration for after the day is won.

#### Martial Masters

The term "fighter" encompasses warriors of all shapes, sizes, and battle styles, from masters of blades, bows, and hammers to clubs, maces, and polearms. Some even use a deadly combination of martial prowess mixed with arcane magic or psionic powers of the mind. It follows then that the foods they prepare and prefer are as distinctive as their fighting styles and implements of war. For example, a XPHB who specializes in complex combat techniques and tactics prefers cooking methods that are equally so. From butchering to braising, precision and skill are the hallmarks of their cuisine. The XPHB, on the other hand, is a creature of pure bravery and brawn, and their dishes invariably have bold elements of flavor, usually along with a few risks that are sure to guarantee glorious culinary victory or the flavors of ignominious defeat.

The XPHB blends their skills on the battlefield with an array of arcane magic and is inclined toward concoctions every bit as complex and potent as the battlefield incantations they cast. While little is known about the tastes of Psi Warriors due to their rarity, it is said that these fighters often cook their victory meals without lifting a finger, yet still the knives chop, spoons stir, and spices shake all through their powers of the mind. Perhaps the most distinctive of all is the Dungeoneer who, by preference or necessity, has come into the habit of preparing freshly slain monsters for their meals—fangs, claws, poisons, and all.

#### Well-Rounded Flavors

The fighter is a survivor in all respects, and this instinct applies not only to the battlefield but also to the wilds. Their exceptional combat skills often carry over to tracking and hunting wild animals and monsters. While not as crafty or precise in the wilderness as the ranger, the fighter is gritty and resourceful and will more often than not get the job done. Whether on the trail or at the tavern, fighters typically prefer meat-based meals, gravitating toward common proteins such as rabbit, deer, pheasant, boar, turkey, ham, rothé, and stirge. However, lighter fare, such as fruits, vegetables, and Chopforest, is also on the table, especially before battle to ensure top performance. Many fighters who have served in the military are well practiced at dealing with meager portions and continual hunger during a siege or march, so they can find themselves contented with simple Iron Rations for extended periods of time.

When it comes to preparation, fighters tend to be practical and watchful, avoiding recipes that are so involved that they leave the party open to ambush. This often means simple, meat-based stews and other single-pan or skillet-cooked meals. When afforded the time, many fighters make excellent butchers due to their exceptional skill with the blade. Consequently, many fighters are known as masters of the grill and spit, creating delicious steaks and roasts from their latest kill.

#### Realms of Battle

No matter where one roams in the multiverse, where there are people, there is conflict. Thus, fighters are numerous on all realms, as natural to each world as its soil and the crops that grow upon it.

Fighters of the oft war-torn Krynn are sure to find comfort and nourishment in the world's many outstanding roadside taverns, such as the Inn of the Last Home, where Otik's Potatoes are so beloved that fighters have been known to re-create this recipe across the multiverse. Bangers and Smash is another tavern favorite, imported directly from the dwarves of Thorbardin of the Khalkist Mountains. Fighters traversing the lands of Solamnia will no doubt find themselves most at home upon the battlements of one of its many great cities or castles, slurping up fresh Solstice Soup and sipping upon the Mulled Wine popularized by Lord Gunthar, Grand Master of the Knights of Solamnia.

The world of Eberron is also no stranger to war, the lands still teeming with fighters both retired and active. Those who dwell in the northeastern military nation of Karrnath often prepare a hearty mushroom dinner bread called Vedbread that can be consumed while on the march. When afforded a short rest, those same fighters delight in a skillet-style meal of eggs, sausage, and red peppers they call the Blood of Vol, named after a popular religious philosophy of self-reliance and the "divinity within." The warriors of Eberron's central Khorvaire nation of Thrane are similarly inclined toward simple, fast, single-pan cooking in their dishes such as Thrakel-Seared Beef in Red Sauce, a flavorful stir-fry of flank steak and fresh vegetables.

Fighters of Toril are as numerous as the stars of Realmspace, delving the world's countless dungeons and lairs in search of untold treasures. Those who delve the notorious Undermountain of Waterdeep are sure to imbibe a tankard or two of Undermountain Alurlyath to gain the "silver glow"—which some insist is the false confidence that comes with drink—before descending to the dungeon's first level from the well in the Yawning Portal tavern. In the unlikely event they return, the newly treasure-laden fighters might celebrate with Honey-Glazed Rothé Ribs or some Spicy Shredded Stirge Sliders. However, great meals are as abundant in the wilds as they are in the world's great metropolises. Accordingly, fighters of this realm need not be orcs to enjoy Orc Bacon or even the Braised Lamb of orcs of the Cold Lands—both common dishes prepared by fighters on the trail to adventure.

Steeped in long traditions of chivalry and heraldry, the fighters and knights of Oerth are much inclined toward celebratory feasts after victorious battles. Venison Sornstag and Hommlet Golden Brown Roasted Turkey are favorites of these castle hearths. However, less fanciful meals are far more the norm, such as the fried egg, sausage, and potato skillet called Quij's Plate, sometimes cooked up in haste on a kite shield. Those who wander too far off the trail and into the mists might eventually find themselves guests at Castle Ravenloft feasting upon Whole Roast Boar even as the gracious host, Strahd, prepares for a meal of his own among the diners.

#### Kitchen Warriors

Whether on the front lines of battle or the kitchen, there are few better allies to have on your side than the fighter. Gritty and practical, they gravitate toward quick, meat-based meals that keep them nourished and satisfied but can make do with whatever the circumstances allow. From light pre-battle snacks to grand post-battle feasts, the food of the fighter is bound to be simple, nutritious, and delicious.

## Monk

> Before you attempt to ride the remorhaz, I might suggest you take a hearty meal. Otherwise, it may be the remorhaz who makes a meal of you. Either way, the cycle of life continues.
> — Cantoule, Grand Master of Flowers

*[Image: image (book/HFLotT/Monk Header.webp)]*

*[Image: image (book/HFLotT/Monk.webp)]*

To a monk, the spirit is a mirror of the body, and through one's physicality the mind and soul are channeled. As such, the body of a monk is quite literally a temple, an edifice where the mortal comes in contact with the divine. The discipline that monks exercise over their body and the control that comes with it are legendary. The cycle of life and the flow of focus from one living body to another make the diet of a monk a serious matter. They dine with purpose, whether alone or with their order, honoring their bodies and their food in ceremonial harmony.

#### From Monastery to Mead Hall

Most monks begin their careers in a monastery, in a communal setting where producing meals is a group effort. Cooking is never drudgery to a monk—it is its own ritual, meticulously timed and requiring the judicious combination of carefully selected components. Monks bring this temperament to their dining in the broader world.

Not all monks eschew blades, but many prefer to work ingredients with their bare hands, having no need for cuts. If a monk must kill to prepare a meal, it is always a considered death. They tend to be unimpressed by trinkets and baubles and have no interest in silver spoons or fancy plates. Many are happy to dine with the simplest implements on the crudest platters.

Sharing a meal with fellow contemplatives in a monastic setting may not be as boisterous as an adventurer's tavern, but it bestows a common appreciation of our dependence on the material world. Monks convey that same appreciation to any meal they share with others. Even those monks who have entirely transcended the need for food and water partake in meals with their friends to feel the kinship of dining together.

#### Meals and Meditations

Monks eat not just to sustain their bodies, but to hone their focus. It has been said that monks have an uncanny metabolism. They choose what to eat in consideration of how they will apply the energy gained; a monk would not wallow in gluttony, partaking no more than is needed. As dramatically as monks can change their own bodies, their powers do not require them to ingest any sort of specific fuel to unlock abilities. It is not the case that monks who favor fiery breath weapons, say, must ingest a bit more pepper and spices, or ginger and garlic—in fact, they might prefer more soothing repasts.

Warriors of the Open Hand tend to seek out more protein-forward dishes, even meats and cheeses, to power their incessant flurries of rapid strikes. The more subtle Warriors of Shadow may favor leaner, more neutral fare like soups and calming teas that steady the hand for delicate, quiet operations. Warriors of the Elements may seek out temperatures and flavors compatible with the elemental affinities they frequently choose. The mask-clad Warriors of Mercy often take their food raw, experiencing its most essential nature.

Powerful monks have been known to willingly ingest the deadliest of poisons, which pose no threat to such an advanced practitioner, but nonetheless offer otherworldly flavors and a strong reminder of the ephemerality of existence. This is a lesson as valuable to those who take lives as to those who save them.

#### The Manifold Path

Even a monk dedicated to an order knows that everyone walks their own path. It is said some monks who live under the strictest vows of poverty eat only what they are given as charity, and thus must eat quite indiscriminately. At the opposite extreme, there are rumored to be monks whose fighting style is based on copious amounts of alcohol consumption.

An itinerant monk may prefer simple fare eaten on the go, even finger food like Goldenstars or Cloaks while in town or foraged salads in the wild. But the most characteristic meals of monks are those made at their temples and monasteries throughout the multiverse.

In the Bloodstone Lands of Faerûn, the initiates serving at the mountainside Monastery of the Yellow Rose lead a hardscrabble life. Working sometimes sixteen hours a day, they tirelessly haul ice from the Great Glacier to be melted down into water and either forage the cliffs or tend to what gardens they can muster. They can, however, produce enough blueberry to make a serviceable Potion of Restoration. And as devotees of FRHoF and St. Sollars the Twice-Martyred, they are well respected and sure to be well fed wherever hospitality is to be found.

In Greyhawk, the sinister Scarlet Order schemes to overthrow the Flanaess in the name of an ancient bloodline. The monks can thus be found infiltrating any institution of importance, where they indulge their appetites as the demands of the subterfuge require. Their hidden monastery, which resides on the southerly Tilvanot Peninsula, is where they hatch their schemes. Observers suspect that the Brotherhood developed their taste for Yuan-Ti Ceremonial Soup from some collusion with Hepmonaland. But it is said monks in Faerûn also favor that refreshing vegetable soup, so there may well be other paths of transmission.

The kalashtar followers of the Path of Shadows who travel in Khorvaire have left behind their monasteries in remote Adar. As fusions of humans with the outsiders known as the quori, the kalashtar mostly reside in their temple keeps, but their desire to do good in the broader world of Eberron leads the most fearless of them to the distant reaches of the world. Some of those who returned brought with them a taste for Blackroot Tal with Honey and the know-how to make it.

#### Culinary Mastery

The life of a monk is one of discipline and structure, of routines and rituals. The primal rhythms of life, breathing, and eating connect the monk with everyone and everything around them. For some, the structures of a monastery guide these activities in productive directions, but others must find their way in the world with nothing but a handful of friends to rely on. The companionship of a monk can make even the most thoughtless eater appreciate the wonders of food.

## Paladin

> You may have forsworn your oath, but your oath hasn't forsworn you.
> — Xenk Yendar, Paladin and Harper

*[Image: image (book/HFLotT/Paladin Header.webp)]*

*[Image: image (book/HFLotT/Paladin.webp)]*

Paladins derive their power from an oath: from uttering a conviction so deep and so certain that it becomes the source of world-altering magic. It may be an oath to defend the helpless, to preserve what matters most, or to avenge something or someone lost. Whatever its subject, the oath takes precedence over all else in a paladin's life, with force that can both smite the wicked and heal the sick. It governs the paladin's interactions with others, their relationship with property, and even their attitudes toward food. No paladin sworn to uphold justice could eat while another goes hungry, and any sustenance taken by a paladin serves as fuel for their oath.

#### Divine Demeanor

Because a paladin's oath takes precedence over their very lives, paladins cannot be self-centered or particularly self-interested. A paladin's grumbling belly is unlikely to be their highest priority: They view dining as a way to nurture others first and themselves secondarily. Paladins never want to take the first bowl or the first slice—indeed, many paladins fast for a day or more at a time in their ascetic devotions. But when they do eat, they make it count.

Although the magic of a paladin is not essentially divine in origin—it comes from within, even when it is divinely inspired—it has much in common with clerical magic. Even novice paladins can learn to purify drink and water and to detect poisons; when sharing a plate with a paladin, you can chow down with confidence. More experienced paladins can even create food and water directly.

Food created by magic can save the day in an emergency, but any paladin would prefer to be more hands-on. When their other responsibilities permit, paladins make for attentive cooks. It has been remarked that paladins are no strangers to blades, and they are more than equal to the task of hunting wild game on their mounts if such is required.

#### Devotion and Glory

Some paladin oaths, or the strictures of their orders, may come with dietary constraints. But otherwise, paladins favor simple, healthy food above lavish dishes. Any paladin will eat heartily to maintain their physical powers, as feats of arms do not come easily on an empty stomach. But for the most part, paladins with access to a larder would serve themselves simple bread, soups, and vegetable-forward dishes, as meats might be considered an extravagance. That said, there are times when ceremony and celebration call for something grander, and paladins understand how inspiring such occasions can be for the people who need inspiration most.

Oath of Devotion paladins set an example of righteousness that all can follow, and that would extend to their choice of meals. A paladin who has taken the Oath of the Ancients would delight in the finest culinary efforts and might be found seeking acclaimed chefs and the most artistic food preparations, but having time-honored and traditional ingredients would be of equal importance. An Oath of Glory paladin might stage a lavish banquet to commemorate a spectacular victory, though it would be a feast mostly intended for others to consume, to help cement the memory of an occasion for the ages.

Although they have a reputation for being stern or even severe, paladins can make very personable company. A meal created and served by a paladin creates its own sort of aura, enlivening the paladin's companions and bestowing them with strength and resilience.

#### Knights in Shining Armor

Many paladins willfully live in poverty and crusade alone as itinerant knights, taking the burden of their oaths entirely upon themselves. But eliminating wickedness from an entire world is not a solo quest. Paladins often find themselves a member of an order that shares and to some degree determines their ideals and goals.

Counted among the Knights of Solamnia in Krynn, there are many paladins and some few branches of knighthood to which they might aspire. The oath that every such knight swears is simple and profound: "My honor is my life." These orders are known for their righteousness, the formality of their etiquette, and their strict codes of conduct. Knights in the Order of Sword must eat nothing for one day each week; Rose Knights must abstain from alcoholic beverages and other perceived vices like gambling. Everything such a knight needs can be found in a simple cup of Solamnic Mushroom Ragout with Dumplings.

The paladins of the Order of the Gauntlet operate throughout Faerûn, acting wherever necessary to counter the influence of the sinister Cult of the Dragon. Any tavern could be their local base of operations, like the Pair of Black Antlers in Elturel, where they might be found dining on Gurdats. It is said the new recruits of the order, who are known as Gallants, keep to the simplest fare, such as Corn and Lentil Soup—their wills bent entirely on defending the weak and smiting evil.

The Purified, as the paladins of ERLW in Khorvaire call themselves, are bound to the Tenet of Purity—a call to "burn the corruption and taint of evil from all Eberron." Many paladins of the Order of Templars skip a meal on the first day of the week, the Day of Cleansing Fire, and the most devout will fast the entire day. But one of the Silver Flame's thirteen high holy days is Baker's Night, when treats and pastries are shared among friends and families of the faithful. A special favorite of that celebration is the Skyway Special, a dish usually reserved for the privileged but available for all when it is time for a feast.

But between those paladins who walk their path alone and those who belong to a grand order, there are many who keep the simple society of a group of trusted friends with whom they adventure, strive, and of course eat. The company of a paladin is not everyone's cup of tea, but those who have the privilege of calling a paladin a friend know that there is no more heartening person to share a kettle with.

#### The Path

The path of righteous is not promised to be a leisurely one; luxury is not often part of a paladin's life. Paladins can spare no time for indulgence and repose while the wicked thrive and the weak suffer. Through their oaths, paladins hope to make the world a better place, a place where comfort is not a stranger to anyone. On the day of victory, they will lead the toast at the celebratory feast.

## Ranger

> The cat wants you to come along. It'll be better than a cave, and better food than half-cooked fish.
> — Montolio, The Ranger

*[Image: image (book/HFLotT/Ranger Header.webp)]*

*[Image: image (book/HFLotT/Ranger.webp)]*

Rangers are the wilderness warriors, scouts, and survivalists of the multiverse. They are also guardians and wardens of the wild, standing vigil on the front lines of settlements to protect from wandering natural threats. Often solitary by nature or profession, they are deeply tied to the land, attuned to its rhythms and resources. And when it comes to food, rangers are more than just self-sufficient; they're artists of the wild pantry. From hunting and foraging to fire-roasted feasts under star-filled skies, rangers help keep companions on course and well fed while on an adventure.

#### Forms of the Hunter

A ranger's talents and magic are honed with deadly focus to protect the world from monsters and tyrants. As such, ranger's tastes and expertise are personified by the very skills they study. A XPHB ranger who shares a mystical bond with their animal companion may expertly track their prey. A XPHB hones their predatory instincts into an art form, knowing just when to strike for a clean, ethical kill. A XPHB not only pursues foes that lurk in darkness, but they are also extremely proficient at procuring uncommon sustenance. And a mirthful XPHB sees the whole world as their larder.

In most parties, rangers serve the role of forager, cook, and quartermaster, responsible for tracking and hunting. In the multiverse, rangers adapt to the cultural culinary traditions of their realms. These regional traditions may even become part of the ranger's identity and are often a bridge to different cultures they encounter.

#### The Green Pantry

From the primal jungles of Chult to the dreamy twilight of the Feywild, ranger cuisine is deeply regional and resourceful. Each world brings unique flora, fauna, climates, and cultures, and rangers adapt their survival and cooking techniques accordingly.

Some rangers follow nonlethal methods when hunting, preferring to trap or capture small game in honor of the balance of nature. Others are unparalleled hunters with an arsenal of options at their disposal, including longbows or crossbows. These survivalists understand the migratory patterns of birds and fish and are adept at identifying ideal game, fat-rich in winter, lean and tender in spring.

As natural explorers, rangers can also forage efficiently in varied terrains, often gathering berries, mushrooms, roots, and herbs. A ranger's knowledge includes not just what is edible but what pairs well.

#### A Taste of the Trail

Rangers are practical cooks, but that doesn't mean their meals are bland. A good ranger can craft a savory stew with nothing more than an old pot, a bed of hot stones, and the right selection of herbs. At their disposal is a myriad of campfire techniques including "Split-Log Fire" in which a halved log with kindling inside burns hot and long for grilling meat; the "Stone Ring Method" in which heat-retaining rocks are arranged in a circle for slow cooking; and the "Clay Bake," an old elven trick that involves encasing meat or fish in mud then cooking it under coals to seal in the flavor.

For a ranger, cooking is a ritual to ground themselves, honor the creatures they've hunted, and bond with allies. Some ranger dishes might include the pragmatic yet flavorful golden-crusted River Fish Fry or Basilisk Broth, which is said to grant temporary resistance to poison (with the right alchemical preparation). When in need of something on the go, a simple Mushroom Jerky, dried over embers and smoked with hickory bark, may well do the trick.

Though not as overtly magical as druids or wizards, rangers do possess spells that enhance their relationship to food and nature. One of their most important survival food spells, goodberry, is a staple for healing and nourishment. Speak with animals enables ethical hunting through consent or gaining knowledge about local game from wildlife. Useful not just for taming companions, animal friendship is a great spell for negotiating with intelligent beasts instead of hunting them. And when time is of the essence, locate animals or plants helps them pinpoint elusive herbs, rare fruits, or specific game creatures.

#### Wild Wanderers

In every corner of the vast multiverse, where civilization thins and wild terrain dominates, rangers learn to live off the land with whatever nature provides.

The continent of Faerûn is a microcosm of the planet Toril's extremely varied climates and cultures. In the Dalelands, rangers forage mushrooms, wild onions, and herbs, cooking easily sourced game in hot stones and ash pits. Elven rangers tend toward lighter, herbaceous fare such as Berry-Glazed Venison. Goliath rangers in the frigid Spine of the World focus on survival staples like Lichen Stew thickened with powdered bone. The Mulhorandi desert rangers of Faerûn roast Spiced Locusts and preserve meat in sun-dried citrus rinds. Rangers in the Chultan jungles survive on massive fruits like jackfruit or durian, fire-roasted grubs, and Spit-Roasted Dinosaur, basted with coconut oil and jungle peppers.

Eberron's fusion of magic and steam-tech influences ranger cuisine. Here, ranger meals can be enhanced by cooking tools or dragonmarked spices. Q'barran jungle rangers might prepare a pot of Crocodile "Thighs and Eyes" Bouillabaisse over a portable arcane stove. Thranes favor food blessed with minor enchantments to resist spoilage or poison. Even Warforged rangers, who usually require no food, often craft meals out of tradition for morale and meaning.

In the Feywild, a plane of inspired beauty where senses are heightened, rangers have developed a dreamlike culinary style. Food here feels alive, sparkling, and often has strange magical effects. In this ethereal realm, meals glimmer with bioluminescence, flowers might bloom into decadent pastries, and butterflies could taste like the sweetest honeyed figs. It's often said that a traveler hasn't truly tasted the Feywild unless they've sampled an ooey-gooey slice of Duskmallow Pie.

The Shadowfell, the antithetical plane of existence to the Feywild, is a bleak expanse of black terrain, twisted forests, and sparse resources. Here, ranger meals are utilitarian but clever, with ingredients including bitter root and creatures like shadow hares or gloom owls. They seek foods that are high in calories, such as smoked meat soaked in shadow vinegar and broth boiled with bone and dark herbs.

In the void between stars, in the region known as Wildspace, rangers aboard spelljamming vessels must get creative. Foraging might entail milking a floating sky-ray, harvesting edible crystal fungi, or scraping nutrient-rich moss off asteroid rock. An astral ranger could serve up Spiced Pork and Orange Peppers for a sojourn across the stars. Here, most meals are lightweight, nutrient-packed, often reconstituted with heat or magic.

#### Wild Soul, Warm Meal

Ranger cuisine is more than survival; it's ritual, resilience, and the quiet comfort of something warm when the world feels cold and overwhelming. To be a ranger is to live with the land not merely on it; to move through the wilds with purpose, respect, and an unshakable bond to nature. This connection extends to the way they eat—practical yet reverent, nourishing both body and spirit. Whether they're snaring pheasants, roasting mushrooms over coals, or conjuring a life-sustaining berry, rangers ensure no one goes hungry...even at the edge of the world.

## Rogue

> What 'tis?! A roadside food stall serving yet more scrumptious morsels in this most delicious of realms?! This I must investigate!
> — Bri'an, Rogue and "Cleric of Tyr"

*[Image: image (book/HFLotT/Rogue Header.webp)]*

*[Image: image (book/HFLotT/Rogue.webp)]*

Masters of stealth, agility, and deception, rogues populate the shadows of the multiverse in search of luxury and loot. While many live on the wrong side of the law, an adventuring party would be foolish to not have one of these scrappy scoundrels around to pick locks, scale walls, find traps, or even orchestrate a con. Their professions tend to be as varied as their skill sets, ranging from simple thieves and pickpockets to cutthroats, assassins, and even tricksters who use petty arcane spells or psionic powers to meet their ends. No matter their specialty, it's usually feast or famine for these resourceful survivors, which applies as much to their day-to-day lives as to the food they consume. The rogue is always in search of their next score or meal and will take everything they can get when they can get it, seeking as much richness in their food as in their purses.

#### Masters of Shadow

The talents of the rogue are many, ranging from sleight of hand, moving silently and picking locks to climbing walls, disappearing into shadows, or unleashing lethal strikes unseen. They are masters of evasion, whether it be the blows of enemies or the law, and some even dabble in illusionary arcane magic or psionic mind powers to enhance their skills of trickery and offense. With such broad skill sets, it is no wonder that their cooking and dining preferences are equally so. For example, those who work primarily as Thieves have grown accustomed to taking what they get and most often find themselves cooking and eating on the run. Ever watchful, they tend to avoid the traps of overcooking, while their dexterity allows them to easily pick up sometimes complicated techniques. Meanwhile, few can butcher steaks, cut vegetables, or slice fruit with the precision of the XPHB. Between their extraordinary skill with the blade and their penchant for surprises, the meals of these killers are sure to include hidden and unexpected flavors that delight.

The cuisine of Arcane Tricksters is nearly as hard to pin down as the scoundrels themselves. Based on their skill and familiarity with arcane magic, they have little trouble following or concocting complicated recipes and usually add some illusory flair as special touch. Like other spellcasters, they typically find help in the kitchen with incantations such as mage hand or will use minor illusions in their presentations to dazzle their diners. The mysterious XPHB, on the other hand, has the habit of using the remarkable psychic blades they can manifest in their preparations even while whispering appetizing suggestions directly into the minds of their diners. The XGE needs no such subtle suggestions in their dining and is far more inclined to eat the meal than prepare it.

#### Shade-Grown Ingredients

Regardless of their profession, the rogue most often lives on the fringes of society. Simple, light, and easy-to-eat produce, such as apples, carrots, plums, peaches, and strawberries, usually pilfered from market carts, is a favorite of these shady professionals who live and work on the run. Iron rations, dried meats, seeds, and nuts play a similar role upon the trail, where practicality trumps enjoyment. However, when time and safety allow, the rogue is known to love preparing and indulging in some of the most decadent meals and desserts of any group. In fact, it is said the secretive banquets held among thieves' guilds are legendary in their flavors and extravagance.

From a preparation standpoint, rogues are often capable of cooking elaborate dishes but always while keeping both their potatoes and eyes peeled. While modest soups and single-pan meals are common dishes on the trail, when given access to a proper kitchen, rogues have a way of surprising the party with a sneak attack of flavor consisting of delicious pastries, cakes, and sweets. In these situations, their tastes go from practical to palatial, favoring sweet and savory flavors ranging from cheese and smoked meats to maple syrup, walnuts, berries, and sweet cream. In this respect, rogues seem to have drawn much influence from halflings, possibly due to all the famous thieves and burglars who have emerged from the species.

#### Food and Flight

Where there is need, there are rogues also. So, it should come as no surprise that these scallywags inhabit every realm of the multiverse. While their inclination is to keep a low profile, their presence is often made known by powerful thieves' guilds that dominate not only the community and culture of rogues but their tastes in food also.

On the world of Toril, the notorious Shadow Thieves of Amn are renowned for their luxurious tastes, having pilfered numerous legendary recipes, from the Honey Ham with Pineapple Gravy of Luiren to the Sour Cream Walnut Cake of the Yawning Portal. Meanwhile, the infamous thieves' guild of Pasha Basadoni in Calimport prefer Fig Cakes while on the move and Double-High Halfling Pie when celebrating their latest heist. On the planet Oerth, the Guild of Thieves of Greyhawk seem to share similar tastes, often wolfing down decadent Halfling Oatmeal Sweet Nibbles while on the lam or on the prowl.

While thieves' guilds are not recognized upon the planet Eberron, their activities are alive and well in the major cities, courtesy of various criminal enterprises and even some of the shadier houses. For example, House Tarkanan supplies their fair share of underhanded assassins and rogues, who often celebrate their successes with the Fruit and Ricotta Pancakes and Silverfruit Pie on offer at the Celestial Vista in Sharn. Far across the multiverse, on the planet Krynn, powerful thieves' guilds are few, yet rogues are many, often gravitating to the decadent treats of the Kender, such as Kender loaf and Kendermore Cobbler. While the Kender themselves often get (unfairly) pegged as sneaks and thieves, there's nothing shady about their food and flavors.

Of course, rogues are creatures of shadow, so it follows that they populate not only every corner of the Material Plane, but its echo planes also. While there's little pleasure to be found in the dismal Shadowfell, the wandering rogues who brave its gray terrain will tell you that the Tavern Crickets and Honey milk make it bearable enough to stay. On the other side of the divide, in the Feywild, mischievous eladrin rogues lose their inhibitions entirely at the sight of a bowl of Seelie Court Cheese and Potato Soup, especially when paired with a decanter of rare Elverquisst. Rogues fleeing retribution or the law via the planes might palm a Chirper's Sugar Biscuit while visiting the Market District of Sigil or a Vanilla Bun from Vada's Otherworldly Goods in the Dancing Night Market.

#### Stealing Bites

Wise adventurers know that having a rogue on board always means fuller purses and fuller bellies. When it comes to rogue cuisine itself, there are really only two types: The kind you survive *on* and the kind you survive *for*. The perennially on-the-run rogue will always endure the former so they can enjoy the latter.

## Sorcerer

> Darling, how can I pick a favorite when I haven't tried everything yet?
> — Deelia

*[Image: image (book/HFLotT/Sorcerer Header.webp)]*

*[Image: image (book/HFLotT/Sorcerer.webp)]*

Magic is a science to be studied for some, but for sorcerers, it is intuition. Raw, wild power waits below the surface to be unlocked and released by the sorcerer's sheer will. Whether the sorcerer feels it to be a gift or a curse, their magic is an essential part of them. The life of a sorcerer is thus a delicate balancing act, channeling the magic within them without ever taming it, mustering the discipline to harness it without letting it consume them. A sorcerer grows by improvising, gradually discovering new facets of their gift sometimes through incremental improvements and sometimes in spectacular breakthroughs. Any chance experience might prove the key to a deeper understanding of themselves—and food is the source of the most primordial of experiences.

#### Side Effects

The self-experimentation so essential to the development of sorcerous magic necessarily involves diet. Some sorcerers may fast for prolonged periods or eat only at odd and irregular hours. Just as some half-forgotten taste or smell can cause childhood memories to flood back, an unexpected combination of flavors might connect deeply with a sorcerer.

Not everyone can be a sorcerer, but a sorcerer can come from any background. As such, the table manners of a sorcerer range from refined to rustic. Some sorcerers are perfectly at home in the finest dining establishments; others are more comfortable in a lowly tavern or a sedate meadow. But then there's always one who takes their tea underwater and upside down. Sorcerers are rare, and as their source of power is within themselves, they can be introverted and solitary. Whether you join a sorcerer for a meal around a king's table or a campfire, you may find it hard to share a menu with them.

#### Expect the Unexpected

Sorcerers may be subject to inexplicable cravings for esoteric ingredients. Out of a desire to overcome their limitations, sorcerers will challenge themselves with food they find distasteful or ingredients that might not strictly be considered edible. A sorcerer might even undertake a quest for the sole purpose of sourcing a rare meat or vegetable or spice—whatever it takes to achieve total self-actualization.

The supernatural side effects that afflict sorcerers may also alter their dining habits, resulting in quirks ranging from minor eccentricities to dramatic shifts in how—and what—a sorcerer might eat. Thus, inviting a sorcerer over to dinner might present some challenges. You might be embarrassed to learn that they suddenly don't eat anything red—and flummoxed when, the week after, they now don't eat anything that is not red. Whatever you put on a sorcerer's plate, you may observe that a bit of prestidigitation has converted it into something else entirely before it goes down the hatch. Sorcerers have a fondness for modifying foods with magic in no small part because some happy accident may lead to an unexpected but quite delicious outcome.

Much of a sorcerer's demeanor and appetite depends on the source of their innate power. Sorcerers whose magic derives from a draconic bloodline may try to align their diet with that of their forbears, which varies, of course, by dragon color but very likely involves copious amounts of fresh meat or anything that goes well with Dragonfire Mead. Aberrant sorcerers may hunger for things eaten on other planes, places where living humanoids were never meant to set foot, where every bite can be its own perilous adventure. Clockwork sorcerers delight in precision and in especially well-timed and judiciously executed meals. And for sorcerers of pure XPHB, anything goes—maybe those iridescent butterflies you just created will turn out to be the most delicious dessert garnish in the world!

#### Worlds of Possibility

Sorcerers are some of the most traveled of adventurers; powerful sorcerers are known to pass among worlds extensively. Not all realms look kindly on practitioners of wild magic, preferring the traditional scholarly wizards of yore. But everywhere sorcerers roam, they can be assured of new mysteries to unlock and new cuisines to enjoy.

Faerûn's sorcerers draw on the raw magic of the Weave, forsaking the intercessors and patrons favored by more conservative spellcasters. The wild magic thus unleashed can be unpredictable, but Faerûn is home to meta-magicians adept at fixing spells on the fly. The most powerful of these sorcerers have developed quite rarefied tastes, traveling far and wide to take in all manner of exotic dishes. The mad sorcerer Emirikol, a famous planeswalker often residing in Faerûn, is said to favor Otyugh Steaks, a delicacy shunned in his home world of Greyhawk.

Krynn is one of several realms that acknowledges sorcery as the most ancient and primal form of magic. The Academy of Sorcery, near the town of Solace, offered tutelage for a time not far from the Inn of the Last Home. Orders like the Legion of Steel are known to employ sorcerers in their campaigns. Many sorcerers in Krynn favor the meditative power of Par-Salian's Tea to turn the mind inward in search of fresh discoveries.

Sorcerers stray to far stranger realms. They may enjoy a Sensate Palate Cleanser while visiting Sigil before they embark on culinary adventures unavailable anywhere else in the multiverse. The sorcerous scholars of Strixhaven indulge in Black Lotus Root. On Eberron, a jaded palate might seek out the dishes made with sparkle mushrooms at the Celestial Vista, like the golden pheasant—full of flavors that would make anyone feel like a favored soul. Even in Avernus there might be a delicacy or two to be found, like Candied Phase Spider Eyes.

#### Adventures in Sensation

There is perhaps no greater culinary adventurer than a sorcerer. But they may not be much for recipes, just as they have no use for spellbooks—to make an experience repeatable seems almost anathema to a sorcerer. But you do need some way to keep track of which exact dishes you've tried and how they might be spiced up. What might inspire nothing but diffidence in one sorcerer might electrify another.

## Warlock

> I wouldn't think of eating something that I couldn't share with her.
> — Mary Greymalkin

*[Image: image (book/HFLotT/Warlock Header.webp)]*

*[Image: image (book/HFLotT/Warlock.webp)]*

True power comes with a price, and warlocks are those who dare pay it. They alone pursue the secret rites that bind them to a patron, a relationship that becomes the font of both wisdom and magic. The pact they forge can take many forms, ranging from apprenticeship to subservience to worship. Patronage is a form of companionship but an unbalanced one: Warlocks are never alone, and someone is always looking over their shoulder. The pact with their patron suffuses every aspect of a warlock's life, including their relationship with food. Some patrons may favor banquets and lavish cuisines; others may encourage temperance or even self-abnegation. A warlock may well find that fare disliked by their patron turns to ashes in their mouth.

#### Bound to Their Appetites

Contrary to popular belief, not all warlocks are thralls to demons, trading their souls away on the installment plan for scraps of magic and power. But no patron extends gifts to a warlock without expecting something in return. Many warlocks believe they have the upper hand in this exchange, that they are on a path to being the true masters. But warlocks act in the world on behalf of their patrons, knowingly or unknowingly; in some perhaps mysterious way, warlocks further their patrons' goals.

It requires a keen analytical mind to discern the very mysteries of the multiverse and a bold spirit to grasp for the hand by which those mysteries were written. Warlocks can be justifiably proud of what they have achieved, and with that may come a certain entitlement. Almost anything could be to a warlock's palate provided it is an elevated dish, something that has earned its place on the table. Some might mistake the refined tastes of a warlock for snobbery, but for all that, warlocks tend to be pleasant dining companions, charming if sometimes supercilious.

As warlocks understand the transactional nature of sacrifice, few among them are vegetarians. Some things must die to fuel the power of others. But a warlock would be unlikely to squander magic for the sake of being fed; warlocks are of a higher station in life than that.

#### Me, Myself, and You

For all their pride in their accomplishments, some warlocks may be accused of taking shortcuts—their path to power is not based on wizardly book learning or sorcerous inspiration but instead on a sort of deal with the devil. As such, warlocks may not always prove the most fastidious of chefs. They tend to be more interested in eating food than in preparing it. If someone cannot be paid to do the cooking, then perhaps someone—or something—can be compelled or summoned to do it.

Much of a warlock's attitude toward food will vary with the nature of their pact. Warlocks with a fey patron may be given to banqueting and indulgence at the table, more for the joy of hedonism than to satiate hunger or thirst. The most austere of warlocks form a bond with a celestial, who would encourage—if not demand—austerity and temperance. Those who contract with a fiend might nervously play at the bon vivant as whispers from the pit encourage them to commit blasphemies and atrocities. Who dares imagine the eldritch fare that a warlock pledged to the Great Old Ones might be forced to endure? Dining with such a patron might involve a shadowy proboscis implanting a sinister egg deep in a warlock's guts, where it will lie dormant...for now.

Those warlocks who keep a familiar might share a meal with it, as the patron's surrogate at the warlock's side—though maybe not if the familiar is a skeleton. But in the company of other mortal diners, a warlock is happy to share their bounty and their company.

#### Planar Pairings

The bond between a warlock and a patron varies in the different realms of the multiverse. In some places where patrons are basically deities, it can be hard to distinguish warlocks from clerics. The eldritch powers of the outer planes could never channel divine magic; yet they can still bestow power on those reckless enough to seek it from them. But because these bonds span planes, warlocks across the multiverse have more in common than other sorts of magic users do: From the perspective of their patrons, all warlocks are drawn from basically the same talent pool.

Fiend patrons are perhaps the most common across the worlds, and their tastes would encourage any warlock to indulge in fare like Fried Fingers. The Abyss and the Nine Hells are indeed places that warlocks are not unlikely to visit over the course of their careers, as when Ammon Jerro found his patron Levistus. A warlock from Faerûn who sits down to a dinner in Avernus with their patron may find a dish like Abyssal Chicken Egg Omelet appeals to both, in different ways.

Greyhawk is unusual in that many potential patrons for warlocks are demigods who still tread the Oerth. The warlocks of Greyhawk might pledge themselves to XDMG, the demonic namesake of a huge northerly region known for its blasted terrain. They're likely to find fare at the numerous taverns outside Iuz's confines. Only the most crazed practitioners of forbidden lore in Greyhawk would dare pledge themselves to the inscrutable likes of XDMG, but those who do might develop an inexplicable craving for Pickled Vine Blight Salad.

#### Just the Two of Us

Only those who've sworn themselves to a patron can truly understand that bond. It is both a push and a pull, a desire to comply and to resist, a source of power, and at the same time, a vulnerable dependence. Whether warlocks seek to emulate their patrons or to counter them, their patron's nature looms over how they comport themselves, how they dress, and how they eat. That is, right up until they have enough power to throw off this yoke—then they'll truly be their own master.

## Wizard

> Marjoram. The name of the spice is marjoram.
> — Raistlin Majere, Master of Past and Present

*[Image: image (book/HFLotT/Wizard Header.webp)]*

*[Image: image (book/HFLotT/Wizard.webp)]*

Wizards are the consummate scholars of magic, dedicating their lives to the study of ancient tomes and the manipulation of the Weave, the unseen cosmic lattice that governs all magical forces. These arcane academics often lead lives of rigorous study, obsessive experimentation, and the occasional jaunt through other planes of existence in their relentless pursuit of knowledge.

#### Arcane Appetites

With their mastery of components (a spell's fundamental ingredients) and their deep respect for arcane "recipes," wizards often approach magic much like the culinary arts. In fact, wizardry and cooking share more than a few parallels: Both require precision, creativity, and an eye for transformation. A wizard's cuisine is an alchemical blend of pragmatism, arcane flair, and imaginative innovation. Their tastes range from the esoteric and extravagant to the ascetic and efficient, shaped by personality, resources, and realm of origin.

Across the multiverse, wizards enchant their meals to enhance flavor, preserve freshness, or even grant restorative magic. A wizard's larder often rivals their component pouch, fully stocked with rare herbs, planar spices, and magically cultivated produce. Some dishes may feature simple fare elevated with a magical twist; others may be wholly otherworldly creations.

#### Culinary Conjurers

Some wizards are so consumed by study that they barely have time for mundane activities such as cooking. They prefer efficient meals with minimal mess, allowing them to return quickly to their spellcraft. These repasts might be prepared by their unseen servant while their master works late into the night. Other wizards, however, embrace cooking with the same enthusiasm they bring to their spellcraft, often delighting in the challenge, the parallels, and the possibilities. To these culinary conjurers, the kitchen is just another kind of laboratory.

Simulacrum sous-chefs may assist in the preparation, and the fire used to cook may even be magically mustered, sometimes adding an extra elemental touch to the dish. However, while many wizards employ magic to streamline the process, like cutting a roast's cook time from hours to minutes, others shun shortcuts in favor of the joy of creation. Magic items like the cauldron of plenty can ingeniously transform water into a nourishing stew from thin air, but such "culinary cheats" can lack the artistry that many wizards crave.

Presentation matters, especially for wizards who often pride themselves on their prestidigitation prowess. An XPHB wizard may add an extra level of flamboyance to the experience, making platters levitate or illusionary garnishes shimmer. If an ingredient is missing, a resourceful AU wizard might simply summon it on the spot. An Abjuration wizard may imbue a meal with added protections to ward off certain evils, while a XPHB might seek to add an incantation that imbues their meals with ability to part the veil of space, time, and consciousness. The wizard makes for a most creative and resourceful chef.

#### A Taste of the Planes

One thing is certain: Wizard food is as unpredictable as the wizards themselves. Their culinary preferences vary wildly across cultures, traditions, and realms.

On Krynn, wizardly diets often reflect the ethos of the Orders of High Sorcery. At the Tower of High Sorcery in Wayreth, White Robes, Red Robes, and Black Robes alike dine on austere meals, simple stews, bread, and tea, echoing their disciplined lives. Dragon Salmon, plated with a red wine reduction, offers up an intelligent protein. But there are also rare delicacies like Moonroot Soup, harvested under the light of Solinari, said to enhance mental clarity. Black Robes, favoring indulgence, may enjoy rich, preserved meats and Bloodberry Wine that symbolize power.

At Strixhaven University, a venerated school of magic on the Plane of Arcavios, the food is as intellectually stimulating as the curriculum. In the Biblioplex Café, the university's central dining hub, the menu changes daily, driven by the chef's whims. Students snack on Mystery Memory Muffins that sharpen their recall of lessons, while fun finds its way onto the plate as well—Prismari Edible Art that melts like chocolate sculptures and Quandrix Fractal Sandwiches that unfold in perfect geometry with every bite.

On Faerûn, in places of arcane study such as Candlekeep, a revered fortress-library perched on the Sword Coast, meals range from practical to posh. Within its cloistered walls, notable eateries cater to the magically inclined. The Hearth is a dining hall–style establishment that serves comfort food capable of arcane replenishment, including Scholar's Stew, a thick lamb and root vegetable medley seasoned with rosemary that is known to stimulate memory. In the House of Rest, a visiting scholar could cap their day with a refreshing Ray of Frost, a cerulean concoction to relax the mind. Further north in Waterdeep's Blackstaff Tower, which serves as both a magical stronghold and training ground, elite mages might find themselves fortunate to indulge on Blackstaff's Delight, a rare dessert reserved for special occasions. This dark chocolate torte, accented with a hint of cayenne and embellished with an edible silver leaf, is said to not only ensorcel one's taste buds but to have been dreamed up by Khelben "Blackstaff" Arunsun himself.

In the arcane-abundant societies of the planet Eberron, culinary magic is practically institutionalized with advanced preparation techniques often enlivening classic concepts. Here, magically concocted cuisine is commonplace, including Cremfels, an infectiously delicious dessert served to over-studied magicians of Morgrave University. Magewrights even specialize in gastronomic magic, preparing meals that restore spell-casting capabilities or calm wild surges such as Soothebrew Tea.

In Sigil, the nexus of the known multiverse, food becomes an interplanar experience. Here, wizard chefs reign supreme in a fusion of cultures and cuisines. With portals to countless realms, ingredients in this planar melting pot range from the mundane to the mystically dangerous. This extraordinary city is a haven for mages in search of rare components and for chefs seeking new frontiers...and frequently, they are one and the same as exemplified in the refined flavors of a serving of Silverquill Squid Ink Pasta with Scallops. Similarly, The Styx Oarsman tavern, situated in the Lower Ward of Sigil, may be notorious for shady dealings, but its Ethereal Soufflé, said to rise under the guidance of a wizard's hand, is equally legendary.

#### Table for One...Thousand Realms

Across the multiverse, wizards command staggering arcane power, but their culinary tastes are just as varied and captivating as their spellbooks. Discerning and often whimsical, a wizard's palate favors subtle twists on the familiar, where creativity and thoughtful experimentation are highly prized. Whether practical or performative, austere or extravagant, wizardly cuisine reveals a universal truth: Even the most brilliant minds need to eat, and when they do, they often bring a little magic to the table.

## Afterword: The Adventure Continues

Every quest ends with a meal—a moment to rest, reflect, and raise a tankard with your companions. As we close out this chapter of *Heroes' Feast*, we invite you to do just that. Whether you found inspiration in a ranger's fireside skillet, a wizard's arcane elixir, or a rogue's late-night indulgence, we hope these class-inspired recipes brought the Dungeons & Dragons experience a little closer to your table and taste buds.

With this book, Heroes' Feast: Legends of the Table, we set out to explore the culinary traditions, personalities, and storytelling potential of D&D's iconic classes—from the stoic discipline of paladins to the wild-hearted creativity of bards. Each recipe was designed not just to nourish but to echo the spirit of its class. We aimed to capture the humor, heart, and heritage of what it means to live and eat like a true adventurer.

This tome joins a growing compendium of *Heroes' Feast* creations: the original Heroes' Feast, a culinary foundation inspired by the peoples of D&D; Heroes' Feast: Flavors of the Multiverse, which includes recipes that traverse the planes and explore D&D's many worlds; The Deck of Many Morsels, a flavorful foray into snacks and sweets; and twenty hour-long episodes of Heroes' Feast, the official D&D cooking show, which brought many of these dishes to life one bite at a time. Together, these projects have built something bigger than any one book—a shared table for fans, families, game nights, and hungry heroes across the realms.

Whether you're a seasoned adventurer or a newly initiated cook, we hope this book offered something meaningful: a moment of creativity, a new technique, or just a reason to gather with your fellowship. The campaign may be over, but the feast goes on. And with a bit of imagination and a dash of courage, the next great meal is never far away.

Until then, may your pans stay hot, your parties stay full, and remember: *Never split the party—host the party!*

We shall see you at the next table.
